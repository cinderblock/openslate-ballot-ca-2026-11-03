p2 City of San Clemente
p2 The People of the City of San Clemente do ordain as follows:
p2 SECTION 1. Title
p2 This initiative measure (Initiative) shall be known and may be cited as The San Clemente Natural Hazards Protection and Resilience Act of 2026
p2 SECTION 2. Findings
p2 The people of the City of San Clemente find and declare the following:
p2 WHEREAS, San Clemente’s unique geography, beach setting, and open space landscapes make it an exceptional place to live, but also expose the City
p2 to risks from natural hazards; the core purpose of this Initiative is to fund local efforts that enhance the City’s resilience by protecting beaches and
p2 wildfire-prone open space, and reduce risk to public safety and public infrastructure; and
p2 WHEREAS, the people of San Clemente seek to take action to protect the City’s beaches, neighborhoods, public infrastructure and future from natural
p2 hazards—specifically wildfires and beach erosion—by funding targeted measures that enhance resilience and mitigate the immediate and long-term
p2 impacts of these threats; and
p2 WHEREAS, San Clemente faces the accelerating natural hazard of beach erosion. Decades of disappearing beaches result from both natural forces
p2 and the disruption of sand flow caused by upstream flood control structures. This ongoing hazard is intensified by powerful storms and the lack of
p2 sand replenishment, resulting in reduced safe beach access and walkable beaches. Urgent action is required, as beach restoration and nourishment are
p2 long-term efforts that require sustained local investment; and
p2 WHEREAS, protecting San Clemente’s beaches delivers lasting public benefits by preserving the City’s character and reputation as a world-class
p2 surfing community, supporting local small businesses tied to the beach economy, and maintaining open, accessible beaches for residents and visitors
p2 alike; and
p2 WHEREAS, our beaches are a cornerstone of San Clemente’s identity—attracting tourism, enhancing property values, and providing families with a
p2 treasured connection to the beach and outdoor living. As rising beach erosion threatens this vital resource, preserving the beach is not only a local
p2 public safety and economic priority; it is also a matter of international significance and civic pride—especially with Lower Trestles selected as the
p2 surfing venue for the 2028 Olympic Games; and
p2 WHEREAS, wide, sandy beaches serve as a natural buffer—protecting the Beach Trail, public infrastructure, and public beach access points from storm
p2 damage; and
p2 WHEREAS, without prompt action, San Clemente risks not only losing the protection provided by wide sandy beaches, but also diminishing its long-
p2 term resilience and quality of life for current and future generations; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City of San Clemente, as steward of its beaches, open spaces, and natural systems, and as guardian of public safety, has an obligation
p2 to act in the public interest by reducing beach hazards and by protecting, preserving, and enhancing the public’s access to, use of, and enjoyment of its
p2 beaches and tidelands for the benefit of current and future generations; and
p2 WHEREAS, City-managed beach hazard protection programs—including sand replenishment, sand retention, and beach and ocean water quality
p2 initiatives—are essential to fulfilling this duty by protecting public health and ensuring safe, accessible recreational spaces for swimming, surfing,
p2 walking, and community events, all of which are core to San Clemente’s beach town identity and local small business coastal economy; and
p2 WHEREAS, Cal Fire’s 2025 Fire Hazard Severity Zone map designates much of San Clemente east of I-5 as a Very High Fire Risk Zone, requiring urgent
p2 action in wildfire risk reduction, flammable brush clearance, and neighborhood-level wildfire prevention; and
p2 WHEREAS, devastating wildfires across California have destroyed lives, homes, and entire communities—underscoring the urgent public benefit of
p2 proactive wildfire prevention, enhanced emergency preparedness, and local fire risk mitigation efforts; and
p2 WHEREAS, San Clemente faces the accelerating natural hazard of wildfire. Thousands of acres of open space across the city are increasingly overgrown
p2 with dense, flammable, and invasive vegetation, creating extreme fire danger near homes, neighborhoods, schools, and critical public infrastructure.
p2 This ongoing hazard is intensified by high wind events, extended drought conditions, and the lack of sustained wildfire prevention and vegetation
p2 management efforts; and
p2 WHEREAS, strengthening wildfire hazard preparedness requires proactive stewardship of open spaces, including vegetation management and
p2 ecological restoration, to reduce fire risk, improve neighborhood resilience, and protect lives, property, and our community’s future; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City of San Clemente, as steward of its natural lands and open space, and as guardian of public safety, has an obligation to act in
p2 the public interest by reducing wildfire hazards. This includes coordinating and implementing wildfire prevention strategies across both public and
p2 private open space. Effective stewardship of these lands—including clearing overgrown vegetation, restoring native plants, and maintaining defensible
p2 space—is essential to protect neighborhoods, public infrastructure, and the lives of current and future residents from the accelerating threat of
p2 wildfire; and
p2 WHEREAS, grant funding for natural hazards protection is uncertain, slow, and highly competitive; protecting San Clemente requires a reliable, locally
p2 controlled source of funding that allows the City to act decisively and meet required cost-share commitments; and
p2 WHEREAS, investing in the long-term resilience of San Clemente’s beaches and wildfire-prone areas protects lives, property, supports public health,
p2 and ensures the community can withstand and recover from future environmental challenges; and
p2 WHEREAS, a legally restricted fund—with clear spending rules, oversight, and public reporting—ensures transparency, protects taxpayer dollars, and
p3 delivers direct public benefits to the community by providing financial resources to address imminent harms from natural hazards; and
p3 WHEREAS, both residents and visitors enjoy San Clemente’s beaches and benefit from local fire protection—this measure ensures that residents and
p3 visitors contribute fairly to preserving what makes our city special; and
p3 WHEREAS, this measure includes a 10-year sunset clause and requires voter reapproval after eight years to extend beyond the initial 10 years,
p3 ensuring accountability and maintaining local control for future generations; and
p3 NOW, THEREFORE, the citizens of San Clemente, by this initiative ordinance, enact the following measure to help keep our community safe, strengthen
p3 our resilience to natural hazards, and protect what makes San Clemente special—by restoring our beaches, reducing wildfire risk, and safeguarding
p3 public health and safety, so that future generations can continue to enjoy the natural beauty, beach access, and quality of life that define our community.
p3 SECTION 3. Purpose and Intent
p3 To protect San Clemente’s beach resources and neighborhoods from natural hazards, this Initiative enacts a legally restricted, locally controlled
p3 special one percent (1%) transactions and use tax within the City of San Clemente, to be administered by the California Department of Tax and Fee
p3 Administration. This Initiative ensures dedicated local funding to protect San Clemente’s beaches and wildfire-prone neighborhoods from natural
p3 hazards—specifically, beach erosion and wildfire risks—strengthening the city’s resilience for current and future generations.
p3 Revenue from the special transactions and use tax shall be allocated equally between the following two legally restricted purposes:
p3 1. Beach Protection – including sand replenishment, sand retention, and beach erosion control; maintenance and repairs of the Beach Trail, beach
p3 access points, and the Pier; and beach water quality protection measures related to ocean stormwater management.
p3 2. Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness – Including proactive wildfire risk reduction efforts, hazardous vegetation management, and
p3 investments in fire emergency response capabilities to enhance public safety and community resilience.
p3 In addition to the purposes described in this Ordinance, up to five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000) of revenue generated by this special tax may
p3 be used to reimburse the City for the actual costs incurred in conducting the elections required to enact or extend this measure. This includes the
p3 initial election and the renewal election referenced in Section 3.22.190 of this Ordinance. Reimbursement shall not exceed the amount invoiced by the
p3 Orange County Registrar of Voters for the portion of election costs attributable to this initiative and shall not exceed five hundred thousand dollars
p3 ($500,000) in the aggregate, shared equally between the Beach Protection Fund and the Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness Fund, and across
p3 both elections.

p3 3.22.020 Operative Date.
p3 “Operative Date” means the first day of the first calendar quarter commencing more than 110 days after the adoption of this Ordinance, the date of
p3 adoption being set forth below.
p3 3.22.030. Purpose.
p3 This Ordinance is adopted to achieve the following, among other purposes, and directs that the provisions hereof be interpreted to accomplish those
p3 purposes:
p3 A. To impose a specific purpose retail transactions and use tax, in accordance with the provisions of Part 1.6 (commencing with Section 7251) of
p3 Division 2 of the Revenue and Taxation Code and Section 7285.91 of Part 1.7 of Division 2, which authorizes the City to adopt this tax Ordinance, which
p3 shall be operative if approved by a simple majority vote of the electors voting on the measure vote to approve the imposition of the tax at an election
p3 called for that purpose, consistent with the people’s constitutional right of initiative.
p3 B. To adopt a specific purpose retail transactions and use tax ordinance that incorporates provisions identical to those of the Sales and Use Tax Law of
p3 the State of California insofar as those provisions are not inconsistent with the requirements and limitations contained in Part 1.6 of Division 2 of the
p3 Revenue and Taxation Code.
p3 C. To adopt a specific purpose retail transactions and use tax ordinance that imposes a tax and provides a measure therefore that can be administered
p3 and collected by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration in a manner that adapts itself as fully as practicable to, and requires the least
p3 possible deviation from, the existing statutory and administrative procedures followed by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration in
p3 administering and collecting the California State sales and use taxes.
p3 D. To adopt a specific purpose retail transactions and use tax ordinance that can be administered in a manner that will be, to the greatest degree
p3 possible, consistent with the provisions of Part 1.6 of Division 2 of the Revenue and Taxation Code, minimize the cost of collecting the transactions
p3 and use taxes, and at the same time, minimize the burden of record keeping upon persons subject to taxation under the provisions of this Ordinance.
p3 3.22.040 Contract with State.

p4 Department of Tax and Fee Administration prior to the Operative Date, it shall nevertheless so contract, and in such a case the Operative Date shall be
p4 the first day of the first calendar quarter following the effective date of such a contract.
p4 3.22.050 Transactions Tax Rate.
p4 For the privilege of selling tangible personal property at retail, a tax is hereby imposed upon all retailers in the incorporated territory of the City at the
p4 rate of one percent (1%) of the gross receipts of any retailer from the sale of all tangible personal property sold at retail in said territory on and after
p4 the Operative Date of this Ordinance.
p4 3.22.060 Place of Sale.
p4 For the purposes of this Ordinance, all retail sales are consummated at the place of business of the retailer unless the tangible personal property sold
p4 is delivered by the retailer or his agent to an out of-state destination or to a common carrier for delivery to an out-of-state destination. The gross
p4 receipts from such sales shall include delivery charges, when such charges are subject to the State sales and use tax, regardless of the place to which
p4 delivery is made. In the event a retailer has no permanent place of business in the State or has more than one place of business, the place or places
p4 at which the retail sales are consummated for the purpose of a transactions tax proposed by this Ordinance shall be determined under rules and
p4 regulations prescribed and adopted or to be prescribed and adopted by the California Department of Tax and Fee Administration or under the Revenue
p4 and Taxation Code.
p4 3.22.070 Use Tax Rate.
p4 An excise tax is hereby imposed on the storage, use or other consumption in the City of tangible personal property purchased from any retailer on and
p4 after the Operative Date of this Ordinance for storage, use or other consumption in said territory at the rate of one percent (1%) of the sales price of
p4 the property. The sales price shall include delivery charges when such charges are subject to state sales or use tax regardless of the place to which
p4 delivery is made.

p6 3.22.150 Use of Proceeds; Expenditure Plan
p6 To provide a public benefit and to improve San Clemente’s resilience to natural hazards by protecting its beach resources and residential neighborhoods
p6 from natural hazards, all revenues collected pursuant to this ordinance from the one percent (1.0%) transactions and use tax shall be deposited into
p6 two legally restricted special funds, maintained by the City of San Clemente as follows:
p6 A. Beach Protection Fund – Fifty percent (50%) of all tax proceeds shall be deposited into a legally restricted fund dedicated exclusively to the
p6 planning, implementation, and maintenance of beach protection and restoration efforts. Expenditures shall be limited to the enumerated purposes in
p6 this section and other directly related activities that clearly advance the goals of natural hazard beach protection and restoration as set forth in this
p6 Ordinance and The San Clemente Natural Hazard Protection and Resilience Act of 2026. The enumerated purposes are:
p6 1. Sand replenishment, nourishment, and retention projects.
p6 2. Beach erosion control measures.
p6 3. Maintenance, repair, and improvement of the Beach Trail, public beach access points, pedestrian bridges, the Pier, and beachfront restrooms—
p6 to ensure safe coastal access, protect critical beach infrastructure, and address damage from erosion and storm events.
p6 4. Beach water quality protection measures related to stormwater management and filtration systems, to keep beaches clean and ocean waters
p6 safe for swimming and surfing.
p6 Of the funds deposited into the Beach Protection Fund, at least seventy percent (70%) of total expenditures must be used for the purposes identified
p6 in items 1. and 2. above. In addition, up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) from the Beach Protection Fund may be expended to
p6 reimburse the City for its proportional share of the actual costs incurred in conducting the elections required to enact or extend this tax, including
p6 the initial election and the renewal election referenced in Section 3.22.190. Such reimbursement shall not exceed the portion of election costs
p6 attributable to this initiative as invoiced by the Orange County Registrar of Voters and shall be limited to a combined total of five hundred thousand
p6 dollars ($500,000) in the aggregate across both funds and both elections.
p6 B. Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness Fund – Fifty percent (50%) of all tax proceeds shall be deposited into a legally restricted fund
p6 dedicated exclusively to fire prevention, emergency response support, and wildfire risk reduction. Expenditures shall be limited to the enumerated
p6 purposes in this section and other directly related activities that clearly advance the goals of wildfire protection as set forth in this Ordinance and
p6 The San Clemente Natural Hazard Protection and Resilience Act of 2026. The enumerated purposes are:
p6 1. Support for fire protection and emergency medical response services provided by the Orange County Fire Authority (OCFA), and/or other
p6 fire protection services provided or contracted by the City of San Clemente, including resources to enhance wildfire readiness, emergency
p6 response capabilities, and the firefighters, paramedics, and equipment needed to protect lives, property, and public safety in San Clemente.
p6 2. City-administered, long-term wildfire prevention programs that include planning, risk assessments, and partnerships to reduce wildfire
p6 hazards across San Clemente. The City shall direct and carry out these programs citywide—on both public and private lands—to remove
p6 flammable vegetation and reduce other fire hazards. Strategies may include mechanical removal, targeted goat and sheep grazing, and
p6 other cost-effective vegetation management techniques that can be applied across large or connected areas. Treated areas may also be
p6 restored with native, fire-resistant plants to help prevent the regrowth of invasive species, stabilize soils, and reduce the risk of future
p6 wildfires.
p6 3. Citywide community outreach, education, and engagement programs focused on wildfire prevention and vegetation management across
p6 both public and private property. This includes homeowner and neighborhood wildfire hazard risk reduction strategies, coordination with
p6 homeowners’ associations (HOAs), neighborhood groups, and community organizations.
p6 In addition, up to two hundred fifty thousand dollars ($250,000) from the Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness Fund may be expended to reimburse
p6 the City for its proportional share of the actual costs incurred in conducting the elections required to enact or extend this tax, including the initial
p6 election and the renewal election referenced in Section 3.22.190. Such reimbursement shall not exceed the portion of election costs attributable to
p6 this initiative as invoiced by the Orange County Registrar of Voters and shall be limited to a combined total of five hundred thousand dollars ($500,000)
p6 in the aggregate across both funds and both elections.

p7 3.22.160 Independent Annual Audit.
p7 The proceeds resulting from these transactions and use tax shall be subject to the same independent annual audit requirements as other City revenues.

p7 3.22.170 Implementation
p7 A. Oversight and Management
p7 The City of San Clemente shall be solely responsible for the implementation, administration, and oversight of all programs, projects, and expenditures
p7 funded pursuant to this ordinance. All uses of revenue collected under this measure shall comply strictly with the purposes and restrictions set forth
p7 herein.
p7 B. City Council Approval Required
p7 All programs, projects, expenditures, contracts, and grant applications funded in whole or in part from the Beach Protection Fund or the Fire Protection
p7 and Wildfire Preparedness Fund shall require approval by the San Clemente City Council at a duly noticed public meeting.
p7 C. Operational Planning Requirement
p7 Within twelve (12) months of the effective date of this ordinance, using moneys deposited into the funds established in section 3.22.150 A and B, the
p7 City shall prepare and adopt operational plans for protecting and enhancing the City’s natural hazards resilience, including:
p7 1. ABeach Protection Operational Planthat identifies project priorities, timelines, geographic targets, cost estimates, environmental permitting
p7 requirements, and long-term goals for beach restoration and erosion control.
p7 2. A Wildfire Protection and Preparedness Operational Plan that establishes a wildfire prevention and risk reduction program, incorporating
p7 defensible space best practices, fire fuel load reduction targets and strategies, interagency emergency coordination protocols, and public
p7 education objectives.
p7 Each plan shall be reviewed and adopted by resolution of the City Council at least once every four (4) years, or more frequently as needed to reflect
p7 emerging risks, funding availability, or new regulatory requirements. Moneys deposited into the funds established in section 3.22.150 A and B may be
p7 utilized for the purpose of updating the plans.
p7 D. Interdepartmental and Interagency Coordination
p7 The City may coordinate with local, regional, state, and federal agencies, as well as with Homeowners’ Associations and community-based organizations,
p7 to implement the goals of this ordinance and maximize the impact of the funds through partnerships and grant leveraging.
p7 E. Administrative and Reporting Duties
p7 City staff shall ensure timely development of required reports, budgets, audits, and public information related to this ordinance, including the

p7 3.22.180 Reporting and Oversight.
p7 A. Annual Accountability Report
p7 The City of San Clemente shall prepare and publish an Annual Accountability Report detailing the use of revenues generated pursuant to this ordinance
p7 from both the Beach Protection Fund and the Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness Fund, to provide full transparency and maintain public trust.
p7 This report shall be presented to the City Council in a public meeting and made available to the public via the City’s website no later than three (3)
p7 months after the close of each fiscal year in which the tax is collected.
p7 B. Report Contents
p7 The Annual Accountability Report shall include, but not be limited to, the following:
p7 1. A full accounting of tax revenues received and deposited into both dedicated funds.
p7 2. A categorized breakdown of expenditures by project, program, or operational activity.
p7 3. A summary of progress and outcomes aligned with the most recent Beach Protection Operational Plan and Wildfire Protection and Preparedness
p7 Operational Plan.
p7 4. Performance data including relevant metrics, milestones, or deliverables.
p7 5. Identification of unspent funds with proposed future uses.
p7 6. Recommendations for improving efficiency, effectiveness, or alignment with voter intent.
p7 C. Oversight Committee(s) Established.
p7 1. Financial Oversight:
p7 The City’s Investment Advisory Committee is hereby designated as the Financial Oversight Committee for purposes of this chapter. The Committee
p7 shall review all expenditures of funds generated by the transactions and use tax established by this ordinance and ensure that all disbursements are
p8 consistent with the legally authorized purposes of beach protection and restoration, and wildfire protection, as defined herein.
p8 An Annual Accountability Report shall be prepared and submitted to the Financial Oversight Committee. The Committee shall review the report,
p8 verify that funds have been expended in accordance with this ordinance, and issue a written Statement of Compliance or Concern. This statement
p8 shall be included in the public record and published alongside the Annual Accountability Report.
p8 2. Operational Oversight:
p8 In addition to financial oversight, the City may establish a separate Operational Oversight Committee to provide periodic review of project
p8 implementation, performance outcomes, and alignment with the City’s wildfire and beach protection operational plans. The structure, responsibilities,
p8 and reporting frequency of the Operational Oversight Committee shall be determined by resolution of the City Council.
p8 D. Transparency and Public Access
p8 All reports, supporting documentation, and Oversight Committee findings shall be posted on the City of San Clemente’s official website and maintained
p8 in a publicly accessible archive for the duration of the tax’s effective period.
p8 3.22.190 Term
p8 If this measure is approved by voters, the sales tax increase shall take effect on the operative date, which is the day of the first calendar quarter
p8 commencing more than 110 days after the adoption of this ordinance.
p8 The measure shall remain in effect for a period of 10 years.
p8 An election shall be held on the question whether the tax imposed by the Ordinance shall be renewed (“Renewal Election”) in the calendar year
p8 beginning eight years after the Operational Date at a general municipal election. If a simple majority vote affirms the measure in the Renewal Election,
p8 it shall be extended for one additional 10-year term.
p8 If the measure fails to receive a simple majority at the Renewal Election, the sales tax increase shall expire at the end of the 10-year period beginning
p8 with the Operational Date, ensuring an orderly conclusion to funded programs and projects.

p9 Measure M
p9 A petition signed by the requisite number of San Clemente voters placed Measure M on the ballot for voters to decide whether a 1% transactions
p9 and use tax (commonly known as “sales tax”) should be added to retail sales prices (or 1 cent for an item that costs one dollar).
p9 Because this measure legally restricts the use of revenue to specific purposes, it is a “special tax”. Half the proceeds must be deposited into
p9 a new City “Beach Protection Fund” and expenditures must directly relate to: (1) sand replenishment, nourishment or retention projects; (2) beach
p9 erosion control measures; (3) maintenance, repair or improvement of the Beach Trail, public beach access points, pedestrian bridges, the Pier or
p9 beachfront restrooms; or (4) beach water quality protection measures. At least 70% of total Beach Protection Fund expenditures must be for items (1)
p9 and (2) above. The other half of the proceeds must be deposited into a new City “Fire Protection and Wildfire Preparedness Fund” and expenditures
p9 must directly relate to: (1) support for fire protection or emergency medical response services; (2) citywide wildfire prevention programs on public and
p9 private lands; or (3) citywide community outreach, education and engagement programs focused on wildfire prevention and vegetation management
p9 across public and private property.
p9 The two dedicated funds will be subject to the same independent annual audit as other City funds. Up to $250,000 from each fund ($500,000
p9 total) may reimburse the City for its actual costs incurred to the Orange County Registrar of Voters for conducting the elections to enact or extend this
p9 tax. The City’s Investment Advisory Committee will serve as an Oversight Committee, reviewing expenditures of all revenue collected to determine
p9 whether funds are properly expended. An accounting of revenues and expenditures will be presented annually to the City Council and made available
p9 for public review. The City will be required to publish an “Annual Accountability Report” including breakdowns, summary of progress and outcomes
p9 aligned with the required Beach Protection and Wildfire Protection and Preparedness Operational Plans, performance data, unspent funds, proposed
p9 future uses, and recommendations for improvements.
p9 Currently, the tax on retail sales within the City of San Clemente is 7.75%, which includes the 7.25% base tax rate and Orange County’s 0.5%
p9 local transportation tax. Of the 7.25% base tax collected by the State, the City of San Clemente receives 1% directly. The City estimates a new 1% sales
p9 tax would result in additional revenue of approximately $15 million annually to the City of San Clemente for 10 years and could be extended for an
p9 additional 10 years by another majority vote in 8 years.
p9 A “Yes” vote is a vote in favor of the tax. A “No” vote is a vote against the tax. Measure M will be approved if it receives a majority (50% + 1) of
p9 “Yes” votes. If approved, the total sales tax rate within the City of San Clemente will increase to 8.75%.
