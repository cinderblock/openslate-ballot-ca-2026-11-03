p2 Sec. 1-2-601. – Definitions.
p2 The definitions contained in this section shall govern the construction, meaning, and application of words and phrases used in this
p2 chapter. Words or phrases used in this chapter that are not specifically defined in this section shall be interpreted in the manner required by
p2 Section 1-1-102.
p2 “Choice” means an indication on a ballot of a voter’s assigned ranking of candidates (i.e., first choice, second choice, third choice, etc.)
p2 for any single office according to the voter’s preference.
p2 “Continuing ballot” means a ballot that counts towards a continuing candidate.
p2 “Continuing candidate” means a candidate that has not been eliminated.
p2 “Majority of votes” shall mean more than fifty percent (50%) of the votes cast on continuing ballots.
p2 “Next ranked” means the highest ranked choice for a continuing candidate.
p2 “Ranked choice voting” shall mean an election system in which voters rank the candidates for office in order of preference, and the
p2 ballots are counted in rounds that, in the case of a single-winner election, simulate a series of runoffs until one candidate receives a majority
p2 of votes. Also known as “instant runoff voting.”
p2 “Round of counting” or “round” means a step in the counting process during which votes for all continuing candidates are tabulated for
p2 the purpose of determining whether a candidate has achieved a majority of the votes cast for a particular office, and, absent a majority, which
p2 candidate or candidates must be eliminated. During the tally of the election, preliminary results of city contests using RCV shall be released at
p2 the same time as all other city contests have preliminary results released.
p2 “Skipped ranking” means a ballot contains a higher ranking with no candidate indicated and contains a lower ranking with a candidate
p2 indicated for a single office.
p2 “Vote” means a ballot choice that is counted toward the election of a candidate. During each round of counting, each continuing ballot
p2 contains one vote. All first choices are votes and lower ranked choices are potential runoff votes that may, in accordance with the requirements
p2 of this chapter, be credited to and become votes for a candidate.
p2 “Voting equipment” means all ballots and/or voting devices, vote tabulating systems and/or similar or related systems to be used in the
p2 conduct of the City’s election, including but not limited to paper ballot systems, optical scan systems, and touchscreen systems.
p2 Sec. 1-2-602. – General Provisions.
p2 A. Except as specified in Section 1-2-611.A, ranked choice voting elections for single-winner city offices shall be conducted according
p2 to the procedures in this Chapter.
p2 B. Ranked choice voting shall commence with all city council member and mayoral elections from and after January 1, 2028.
p2 C. Ranked Choice Voting shall not preclude the City from consolidating its municipal elections with the County.
p2 Sec. 1-2-603. – Ballot.
p2 A. The ranked choice voting ballot shall allow voters to rank at least five choices. The City Council may set a higher number of rankings
p2 by resolution.
p2 B. The ballot shall not interfere with a voter’s ability to rank a write-in candidate.
p3 C. Instructions provided to voters shall conform substantially to the following specifications but may be modified based on ballot
p3 design and voting equipment used:
p3 “Vote by ranking candidates in your order of choice. You may rank as many or as few candidates as you choose. Select a different
p3 candidate for each ranking. Select only one candidate in each ranking. Ranking more candidates will not hurt your higher ranked
p3 candidates. Do not skip rankings. In the event you do skip a ranking, your vote will be given to your next continuing candidate.”
p3 Sec. 1-2-604. – Tabulation.
p3 The ballots shall be counted in rounds.
p3 A. In the first round, every ballot shall count as a vote towards the first-choice candidate; provided, however, that if no first choice
p3 candidate is indicated on a ballot, then that ballot shall count as a vote towards its next ranked candidate.
p3 B. After any round, if any candidate receives a majority of votes from the continuing ballots, that candidate shall be declared the
p3 winner.
p3 C. If no candidate receives a majority, the candidate receiving the fewest number of votes shall be eliminated.
p3 D. Every ballot counting towards the eliminated candidate shall be advanced to the next ranked continuing candidate. All the
p3 continuing ballots for all continuing candidates shall be counted again in each new round.
p3 Sec. 1-2-605. – Ties.
p3 If two or more candidates tie for the fewest number of votes, the candidate to eliminate shall be chosen by lot.
p3 If there are only two candidates in the final round and they are tied, the winner will be the candidate who received the higher number
p3 of votes in round one.
p3 Sec. 1-2-606. – Skipped Rankings; Multiple Rankings for One Candidate.
p3 Consistent with Section 1-2-604.D, in the first or any round, if any ballot reaches a ranking with no candidate indicated, that ballot
p3 shall immediately be advanced to the next ranking; If a voter ranks a single candidate more than once, only the highest ranking given to that
p3 candidate is accepted, and each lower ranking for that same candidate is ignored as if it were a skipped ranking.
p3 Sec. 1-2-607. – Elimination of Undervotes, Overvotes, and Exhausted Ballots.
p3 For purposes of this Section and Section 1-2-608, the following definitions shall apply:
p3 A. “Undervote” means a ballot that is blank and has no candidates indicated at any ranking.
p3 B. “Overvote” means a ballot which indicates a vote for more than one candidate in the applicable round of voting.
p3 C. “Exhausted” means a ballot that cannot be advanced to the next round because no further continuing candidates are ranked on the
p3 ballot.
p3 For each round, all ballots that qualify as undervote, overvote, or exhausted shall not count toward any candidate, nor shall such ballots be
p3 counted in any further round.
p3 Sec. 1-2-608. – Reports.
p3 The following reports shall be produced for public review.
p3 A. The “summary report” for a contest shall mean a report that lists the candidate vote totals in each round, and the cumulative
p3 numbers of undervotes, overvotes, and exhausted ballots in each round.
p3 B. The “ballot cast report” for a contest shall mean a report that lists, for each ballot, the candidate or candidates indicated at each
p3 ranking, the precinct of the ballot, and whether the ballot was cast by a vote-by-mail ballot. In the report, the ballots shall be
p3 listed in an order that does not permit the order in which they were cast in each precinct to be reconstructed.
p3 C. The “comprehensive report” for a contest shall mean a report that lists the vote totals in the summary report by precinct. The
p3 report shall list, for each round, the number of ballots cast in each precinct that:
p3 1. were tallied as votes for each candidate in that round,
p3 2. have been declared undervotes,
p3 3. have been declared overvotes, cumulatively for all previous rounds and inclusive of the reported round of tabulation, and
p3 4. have been declared exhausted cumulatively for all previous rounds and inclusive of the reported round of tabulation.
p3 D. Mode and manner of release. Preliminary versions of the summary report and ballot image report shall be made available as
p3 soon as possible after the commencement of the canvass of votes cast. The summary report, ballot image report, comprehensive
p3 report, and preliminary versions of the summary report and ballot image report shall be made available to the public during the
p3 canvass via the Internet and by other means. The ballot image report and preliminary versions of the ballot image report shall
p3 be made available in a plain text electronic format. In any case, preliminary versions of these reports shall be made available to
p3 the public prior to the commencement of the manual tally.
p4 Sec. 1-2-609. – Continuing the Tally to Two Candidates.
p4 If a winner is declared when there are three or more continuing candidates (including the winner), and if the vote tabulating system
p4 allows for it, additional rounds of tallying shall occur until there are only two candidates left for reporting purposes only.
p4 Sec. 1-2-610. – Manual Tally.
p4 A. A preliminary version of the comprehensive report shall be made available to the public prior to the selection of precincts for
p4 the public one percent manual tally, as provided by state law.
p4 B. After each round of the manual tally, the second and third choice votes shall be assigned based on the candidate totals in the
p4 summary round-by-round report for the entire contest.
p4 Sec. 1-2-611. – Election Procedures if Ranked Choice Voting is Not Feasible.
p4 A. If the voting equipment cannot feasibly accommodate ranked choice voting, then the City shall continue to use the plurality
p4 voting procedures that were used prior to January 1, 2028, until such time as the voting equipment can feasibly accommodate
p4 ranked choice voting.
p4 B. If the voting equipment cannot feasibly accommodate all of the procedures in section 1-2-601 through 1-2-610, the City Clerk
p4 may make changes to those procedures provided that ranked choice voting shall still be used and the smallest feasible number
p4 of changes made until such time as the voting equipment can accommodate those procedures in their entirety.
p4 C. If the Secretary of State changes its currently adopted Instant Runoff Voting Guidelines (“Instant Runoff Voting in Charter
p4 Counties and Charter Cities”) for the conduct of ranked choice voting elections and the voting equipment used to conduct the
p4 County of Orange’s elections can accommodate the changes in the State’s guidelines, the City Clerk shall have the option of
p4 adopting those guidelines, in whole or in part, in lieu of the ranked choice voting procedures in this section.
p4 D. Managing the Cost of RCV for Irvine
p4 1. For purposes of this sub-section 1-2-611(D) the following definitions shall apply:
p4 i. “Estimated Election Cost” means the City’s adopted budget for the next election that will include a single-winner city
p4 office.
p4 ii. “RCV Cost” means the additional cost to the city above and beyond the latest currently provided Estimated Election Cost
p4 for the next non-ranked choice voting election that will include a single-winner city office.
p4 iii. “RCV Cost Limit” means 0.23% of the then-current total city general fund budget for the fiscal year within which the
p4 general election will be held.
p4 2. The RCV Cost shall be calculated by adding (i) the average of the low and high range estimate from the Orange County
p4 Registrar of Voters regarding the County of Orange’s additional costs that will be billed to the city for the use of ranked
p4 choice voting at the next scheduled election that includes a single-winner city office to (ii) an estimate from the City Clerk
p4 of the additional City costs to be incurred for the use of ranked choice voting at the next scheduled election that includes a
p4 single-winner city office.
p4 3. If the RCV Cost is greater than the RCV Cost Limit, then the City Council shall not transition to ranked choice voting at the
p4 next council member and/or mayoral election. The comparison of RCV Cost to RCV Cost Limit shall occur prior to each
p4 subsequent council member and/or mayoral election until the City transitions to ranked choice voting.
p4 4. Once a ranked choice voting election has occurred during any election cycle in the city, ranked choice voting shall continue
p4 to be used at every subsequent election unless the voters replace it with another system. This sub-section 1-2-611(D) only
p4 applies to the first usage of ranked choice voting.

p5 Measure Directing the Use of Ranked Choice Voting for City Council Member and
p5 Mayoral Elections After January 1, 2028
p5 The City Council of the City of Irvine has placed a measure before the voters that would amend the Irvine Municipal Code to use ranked choice voting
p5 (RCV) for City Council member and mayoral elections after January 1, 2028, if certain technical and financial conditions are satisfied.
p5 The City of Irvine (City) currently uses a plurality voting system for City Council member and mayoral elections. Under that system, voters vote for only
p5 one candidate and the person receiving the most votes wins the election even if they do not receive a majority of the votes.
p5 In RCV elections, voters rank their choices in order of preference, such as first, second, third, and so on. If no candidate wins a majority of first-place
p5 votes, the candidate with the fewest votes is eliminated, and ballots ranking that candidate are transferred to the next-ranked continuing candidate.
p5 This process repeats until one candidate secures a majority of the votes counted in that round.
p5 Under the proposed measure, the City would transition to RCV after January 1, 2028, once all of the following conditions are satisfied:
p5 • The available voting equipment can feasibly accommodate RCV;
p5 • Transitioning to RCV will not prevent the consolidation of City elections with elections conducted by the County of Orange; and
p5 • The additional cost for an RCV election is not more than 0.23% of the City’s general fund budget for the fiscal year during which the proposed
p5 RCV election would be held (RCV Cost Cap). For reference, 0.23% of the City’s fiscal year 2026-2027 general fund budget of approximately
p5 $312.1 million is approximately $717,790. However, the actual RCV Cost Cap would be calculated using the general fund budget for the fiscal
p5 year in which the proposed RCV election would be held.
p5 When the above conditions are met and the City transitions to RCV, the City will continue to conduct elections by RCV at every City Council member
p5 and mayoral election unless the voters choose another voting system.
p5 The proposed measure requires that RCV ballots allow the ranking of a minimum of five candidates, that write-in candidates be permitted, and that
p5 voters receive specific instructions regarding RCV. The proposed measure also establishes procedures for tabulating votes, resolving ties, resolving
p5 voting mistakes (e.g., skipping a ranking, or giving the same ranking to more than one candidate), conducting a manual vote tally, and providing reports
p5 for public review. The proposed measure authorizes the City Clerk to change those procedures if (1) the voting equipment will not feasibly accommodate
p5 the voting procedures, or (2) the Secretary of State changes its current RCV guidelines and adopts new guidelines that can be accommodated by the
p5 voting equipment.
p5 A “Yes” vote is in favor of adopting the measure and transitioning to RCV elections after January 1, 2028, if the specified technical and
p5 financial conditions are met.
p5 A “No” vote is against adopting the measure.
p5 If a majority of voters vote “Yes,” then this measure will take effect ten (10) days after election results are declared by the City Council.
