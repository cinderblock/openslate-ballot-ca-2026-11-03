p2 City of Orange
p2 SIMPLE HOME RULE CHARTER
p2 City of Orange, California
p2 We, the people of the City of Orange, seeking to preserve local control, strengthen fiscal responsibility, protect taxpayer resources, and ensure that
p2 decisions affecting our community are made by the people closest to those decisions, do hereby adopt this Charter under the Constitution of the State
p2 of California.
p2 It is our intent to establish a flexible Home Rule Charter that preserves the Council-Manager form of government, maintains transparency and
p2 accountability, protects public safety and neighborhood quality of life, and provides the City with greater authority over municipal affairs including
p2 contracting, procurement, elections, and local governance.
p2 This Charter shall be interpreted broadly in favor of municipal self-governance and local control.
p2 ARTICLE I
p2 INCORPORATION AND POWERS
p2 Section 100. Name
p2 The City of Orange shall continue to be a municipal corporation under its present name of the “City of Orange.”
p2 Section 101. Boundaries
p2 The boundaries of the City shall continue as established unless changed in the manner authorized by law.
p2 Section 102. Powers
p2 The City shall have all powers possible for a Charter City under the Constitution and laws of the State of California, including the full power to make
p2 and enforce all laws and regulations with respect to municipal affairs.
p2 The enumeration of powers in this Charter shall not be deemed exclusive and shall not restrict the authority of the City to govern local affairs.
p2 Section 103. Continuation of Existing Laws
p2 All lawful ordinances, resolutions, rules, regulations, contracts, rights, obligations, and liabilities existing at the time this Charter takes effect shall
p2 remain in full force unless modified pursuant to this Charter.
p2 ARTICLE II
p2 MUNICIPAL AFFAIRS AND GENERAL LAWS
p2 Section 200. Municipal Affairs
p2 The City of Orange has the power to make and enforce all laws respecting its municipal affairs as described by this Charter and established by
p2 the California Constitution, California statutes, and judicially defined law. This power is limited only as set forth in this Charter and the California
p2 Constitution.
p2 Such powers are in addition to those granted or prescribed by applicable law for municipal corporations within the State of California. Each responsibility
p2 of governance set forth in this Charter is declared to be a municipal affair or concern.
p2 The enumeration by this Charter of any particular power shall not constitute a limitation upon the City’s general grant of powers under applicable law.
p2 Section 201. General Laws
p2 In addition to the powers granted by this Charter and the California Constitution, the City has the power and authority to adopt, make, exercise, and
p2 enforce all legislation, laws, and regulations and to take all actions and exercise any and all rights, powers, and privileges allowed by law. To the fullest
p2 extent permitted by the California Constitution, in the event of a conflict regarding a municipal affair, the provisions of this Charter shall control.
p2 Section 202. Enactment of Laws and Exercise of Authority
p2 Elected and appointed officials who exercise the powers granted by this Charter are prohibited from favoring or unlawfully discriminating against
p2 persons based upon a legally protected classification.
p2 When enacting laws pursuant to this Charter, the City Council shall include findings that such laws promote public health, safety, welfare, fiscal
p2 responsibility, and the long-term interests of the residents of the City of Orange.
p2 Section 203. Land Use
p2 Except as otherwise provided by ordinance of the City Council, the City shall continue to be governed by California law as it applies to general law
p2 cities with respect to land use matters, including without limitation General Plan consistency, zoning regulations, subdivision laws, and housing law
p2 compliance.
p2 Section 204. Open Space Reserve Protection
p2 Establishment of the Open Space Reserve.
p2 The Open Space Reserve is hereby established to preserve the City’s significant open space lands, parks, hillsides, canyons, creek corridors, wildlife
p3 habitat, scenic vistas, equestrian character, trails, and other natural resources for the benefit of present and future generations. The Open Space
p3 Reserve shall consist of all real property designated by ordinance or resolution of the City Council as part of the Open Space Reserve, together with
p3 any property thereafter so designated.
p3 Permitted Uses.
p3 Property within the Open Space Reserve shall be maintained and used exclusively for open space preservation, passive recreation, parks, trails,
p3 equestrian uses, habitat restoration, conservation, watershed protection, wildlife protection, educational uses, agriculture, grazing, public safety,
p3 public utilities, flood control facilities, and other public improvements or uses that are compatible with and preserve the property’s natural, scenic,
p3 recreational, environmental, or rural character.
p3 Prohibited Uses.
p3 The City shall not approve, construct, permit, lease, sell, transfer, redesignate, subdivide, or otherwise authorize any residential, commercial, industrial,
p3 or mixed-use development within the Open Space Reserve that is inconsistent with the purposes of this Section. This prohibition includes, but is
p3 not limited to, single-family residential, multifamily residential, mixed-use residential, affordable housing, supportive housing, transitional housing,
p3 accessory dwelling units, mobile homes, or any other form of residential development.
p3 Removal from the Open Space Reserve.
p3 No property included within the Open Space Reserve may be removed from the Reserve, redesignated for another use, sold, exchanged, leased for
p3 an inconsistent purpose, or otherwise disposed of unless first approved by a majority of the electors voting on the measure at a regularly scheduled
p3 municipal election.
p3 Consistency Finding.
p3 Before approving any improvement or use within the Open Space Reserve, the City Council shall find that the proposed action is consistent with the
p3 purposes of this Section and will not materially impair the natural, scenic, recreational, environmental, or conservation value of the property.
p3 Emergency and Maintenance Activities.
p3 Nothing in this Section shall prohibit emergency response activities or the repair, maintenance, replacement, or improvement of existing public
p3 facilities, trails, utilities, flood control facilities, or other public infrastructure, provided such activities are reasonably necessary to protect public health,
p3 safety, welfare, or the long-term stewardship of the Open Space Reserve.
p3 Replacement Open Space.
p3 If the electors approve the removal of property from the Open Space Reserve, the City Council shall, to the maximum extent feasible, acquire or
p3 permanently preserve replacement open space of equal or greater public, recreational, environmental, or conservation value.
p3 Permanent Protection.
p3 The restrictions contained in this Section shall run with the land. This Section may be amended or repealed only by a majority vote of the electors of
p3 the City of Orange voting on the measure at a regularly scheduled municipal election.
p3 ARTICLE III
p3 FORM OF GOVERNMENT
p3 Section 300. Council-Manager Form of Government
p3 The municipal government established by this Charter shall be known as the Council-Manager form of government.
p3 Section 301. Powers Vested in City Council
p3 All powers of the City shall be vested in the City Council except as otherwise provided by this Charter or by law.
p3 ARTICLE IV
p3 CITY COUNCIL
p3 Section 400. Composition
p3 The City Council shall consist of the Mayor and the number of Council Members established by ordinance.
p3 The Mayor and Council Members shall be elected in the manner established by ordinance and in compliance with applicable state and federal law.
p3 If district-based elections are utilized, the City Council shall establish and adjust district boundaries as required by law.
p3 The manner of election, district boundaries, terms of office, qualifications, vacancies, and related election procedures shall be established by ordinance.
p3 Section 401. Mayor
p3 The Mayor shall serve as the presiding officer of the City Council and shall have all rights, powers, and duties of a Council Member.
p3 Section 402. Mayor Pro Tempore
p4 The City Council shall annually select one of its members to serve as Mayor Pro Tempore.
p4 Section 403. Eligibility
p4 No person shall be eligible to hold the office of Mayor unless he or she is, and has been for at least thirty (30) days immediately preceding the filing of
p4 nomination papers or appointment, a registered voter and resident of the City.
p4 No person shall be eligible to hold the office of Council Member unless he or she is, and has been for at least thirty (30) days immediately preceding the
p4 filing of nomination papers or appointment, a legally registered voter and resident of the City and, if elected by district, a registered voter and resident
p4 of the district from which he or she seeks election or appointment.
p4 Each elected Mayor and Council Member shall remain a registered voter and resident of the City, and each Council Member elected by district shall
p4 remain a registered voter and resident of the district represented, throughout the term of office. Loss of any required qualification shall create a
p4 vacancy in accordance with applicable law.
p4 No creation of a district or change to district boundaries shall terminate the term of any Council Member elected prior to such change.
p4 ARTICLE V
p4 TERM LIMITS
p4 Section 500. Mayor
p4 No person shall serve as Mayor for more than two four-year terms in a lifetime.
p4 Section 501. Council Members
p4 No person shall serve as a member of the City Council for more than three four-year terms in a lifetime.
p4 Section 502. Partial Terms
p4 Service of more than two years of an unexpired term shall be considered a full term for purposes of term limits.
p4 Section 503. Application of Term Limits
p4 Pursuant to California Government Code Section 36502, the term limits established by this Article shall apply prospectively from the effective date of
p4 this Charter.
p4 Service as Mayor or Council Member completed before the effective date of this Charter shall not be counted for purposes of calculating the term limits
p4 established by this Article.
p4 No term being served on the effective date of this Charter shall be shortened or terminated by the adoption of this Article.
p4 Only terms of office commencing after the effective date of this Charter shall count toward the term limits established by this Article.
p4 ARTICLE VI
p4 MUNICIPAL AFFAIRS, PROCUREMENT, AND LOCAL CONTROL
p4 Section 600. Procurement and Contracting
p4 The City shall have full authority over municipal contracting procedures, public works procurement, bidding thresholds, design-build authority, best-
p4 value procurement, alternative project delivery methods, professional services selection, and all other contracting methods allowed to Charter Cities
p4 under the California Constitution, including authority to establish exemptions from state public contracting statutes where permitted to charter cities.
p4 Such procedures may be established, modified, or waived by ordinance or resolution of the City Council where permitted by law.
p4 Section 601. Prevailing Wage
p4 The City Council may establish by ordinance the procedures applicable to public works contracting and prevailing wage requirements for municipal
p4 affairs consistent with constitutional authority and applicable law.
p4 Nothing in this section shall be construed to prohibit the City from applying state prevailing wage requirements where required by law or where
p4 determined by the City Council to be in the public interest.
p4 Section 602. Eminent Domain
p4 The City may not exercise the power of eminent domain to acquire private property for conveyance to another private party without the owner’s
p4 consent, except where necessary for mitigation of impacts caused by a public project or for the development, operation, or maintenance of facilities
p4 owned or operated by the City.
p4 Section 603. Protection of Private Property Rights
p4 Except as otherwise required by the California Constitution or applicable state or federal law, the City shall not adopt, impose, or enforce any ordinance,
p4 resolution, regulation, or other legislative or administrative action that establishes mandatory controls on the price, rent, or other financial consideration
p5 payable in connection with the sale, lease, rental, exchange, or other transfer of privately owned residential real property.
p5 Nothing in this Section shall be construed to:
p5 (a) Limit the City’s authority to regulate land use, zoning, planning, subdivision, development standards, or building regulations;
p5 (b) Limit the City’s authority to enter into voluntary development agreements, affordable housing agreements, or other contractual arrangements
p5 authorized by law;
p5 (c) Prohibit the City from implementing or enforcing requirements expressly mandated by state or federal law;
p5 (d) Affect the City’s authority to acquire, own, lease, sell, or dispose of property or to exercise the power of eminent domain as otherwise authorized
p5 by this Charter or applicable law;
p5 (e) Apply to any dwelling unit or accommodation in any hotel, motel, or other facility when the transient occupancy of that dwelling unit or
p5 accommodation is subject to a transient occupancy tax.
p5 Any provision of the Orange Municipal Code inconsistent with this Section is repealed to the extent of such inconsistency.
p5 Section 604. Sales of City Assets
p5 Pursuant to Government Code Section 37350, the City may purchase, lease, receive, hold, and dispose of real and personal property for the common
p5 benefit consistent with all notice, hearing, and voter approval requirements required by law.
p5 Section 605. Public Utilities
p5 Pursuant to applicable law, the City Council may sell and dispose of any public utility owned by the City, subject to all requirements of state law and
p5 voter approval where required.
p5 Section 606. Liberal Construction
p5 This Charter shall be liberally construed in favor of the exercise of authority by the City over municipal affairs.
p5 The powers granted by this Charter are intended to be permissive rather than restrictive, and the enumeration of any specific power shall not be
p5 construed as limiting the general authority of the City to govern local affairs.
p5 ARTICLE VII
p5 VACANCIES IN OFFICE
p5 Section 700. Permanent Vacancy
p5 In the event of a vacancy in an elected office, as defined by California Government Code Section 1770, the City Council shall fill the vacancy by
p5 appointment within sixty (60) days or call a special election in accordance with applicable law.
p5 The person appointed or elected to fill the vacancy shall serve the remainder of the unexpired term.
p5 Notwithstanding the California Elections Code, a person appointed to fill a vacancy who seeks election to the same office in a subsequent general
p5 municipal election shall not be required to use the word “Appointed” in conjunction with the title of that City office as part of the ballot designation
p5 filed in the nomination documents.
p5 Section 701. Temporary Vacancy, Suspension, and Ethical Misconduct
p5 Pending trial or formal proceedings, the City Council may, by a three-fourths vote of the full City Council, suspend any elected or appointed officer
p5 against whom felony criminal proceedings have formally commenced, or where formal judicial or administrative proceedings involving corruption,
p5 fraud, abuse of office, official misconduct, or violations of ethics and conflict-of-interest laws are pending and suspension is authorized by applicable
p5 law.
p5 By a majority vote of the full City Council, the City Council may terminate the suspension at any time and restore the officer to office.
p5 The suspension shall automatically terminate, and the officer shall be restored to office, upon acquittal, dismissal of charges by the prosecuting
p5 authority, dismissal of charges by a court of competent jurisdiction, or final resolution of the matter without disqualification from office. In the event
p5 of a hung jury, the City Council may, by a three-fourths vote of the full City Council, extend the suspension unless the prosecuting authority confirms
p5 that charges will not be refiled. Nothing in this section shall authorize removal from office except where disqualification occurs by operation of law,
p5 including conviction of specified crimes, judicial findings of official misconduct, or violations of ethics and conflict-of-interest laws where removal is
p5 authorized by applicable law.
p5 The City Council may adopt by ordinance additional procedures consistent with due process, applicable law, and this Charter.
p5 Any action taken pursuant to this section shall be subject to review by the City Attorney and shall be consistent with all applicable provisions of state
p5 law.
p6 ARTICLE VIII
p6 FISCAL RESPONSIBILITY
p6 Section 800. Fiscal Policy
p6 The City shall be managed in a fiscally prudent manner that promotes long-term financial sustainability, responsible stewardship of public resources,
p6 and the preservation of the City’s financial capacity to provide essential municipal services to both current and future residents. In exercising its fiscal
p6 authority, the City Council shall strive to maintain the City’s financial integrity while investing in public infrastructure, community priorities, and other
p6 long-term municipal needs.
p6 Section 801. Balanced Budget
p6 The City Council shall adopt a balanced budget each fiscal year.
p6 Nothing in this Section shall prohibit the appropriation or expenditure of accumulated reserves, dedicated funds, grants, impact fees, or other legally
p6 available revenues for capital improvements, infrastructure, equipment, or other one-time expenditures under a pay-as-you-go financing strategy,
p6 even if total expenditures exceed total revenues in a particular fiscal year, provided the City’s ongoing operating budget remains balanced.
p6 Section 802. Independent Audit
p6 An independent annual audit shall be conducted and presented to the City Council and made available to the public.
p6 Section 803. Fiscal Priorities for Additional General Fund Revenues
p6 To promote the long-term fiscal sustainability of the City, the City Council shall give priority to the use of additional recurring General Fund revenues
p6 for the following purposes:
p6 (a) Reducing unfunded pension and other post-employment benefit liabilities;
p6 (b) Repairing, replacing, and maintaining public infrastructure and capital assets;
p6 (c) Reducing outstanding debt and other long-term financial obligations of the City;
p6 (d) Replenishing and maintaining prudent financial reserves; and
p6 (e) Funding one-time capital improvements, equipment, technology, and other non-recurring investments that improve the City’s long-term financial
p6 condition.
p6 Nothing in this Section shall prohibit the City Council from appropriating revenues for essential municipal services or other lawful governmental
p6 purposes, provided the City’s operating budget remains balanced in accordance with this Charter.
p6 ARTICLE IX
p6 LEGAL PROVISIONS
p6 Section 900. Violations
p6 A violation of this Charter or any ordinance of the City shall be subject to an administrative citation and/or misdemeanor in accordance with this
p6 Charter and the Orange Municipal Code.
p6 Section 901. Amendment
p6 Amendments to this Charter shall be proposed and submitted to the electors of the City in the manner provided by the Constitution of the State of
p6 California and applicable law.
p6 Section 902. Definitions
p6 “City” means the City of Orange.
p6 “Open Space Reserve” means those City-owned properties designated by ordinance or resolution of the City Council pursuant to Section 204.
p6 “Shall” is mandatory and “may” is permissive.
p6 Section 903. Severability
p6 If any provision of this Charter, or its application to any person or circumstance, is held invalid or unconstitutional by a court of competent jurisdiction,
p6 such invalidity shall not affect the remaining provisions or applications of this Charter that can be given effect without the invalid provision or
p6 application. To this end, the provisions of this Charter are declared to be severable.
p6 Section 904. Effective Date
p6 This Charter shall take effect upon voter approval and filing with the Secretary of State as required by law.
p7 Impartial Analysis
p7 City of Orange
p7 Measure K
p7 The City Council of the City of Orange is submitting Measure K to the voters for consideration at the November 3, 2026 General Municipal Election. If
p7 approved by a majority of the voters, Measure K would adopt the proposed Charter for the City of Orange, changing the City from a general law city to
p7 a charter city pursuant to Article XI of the California Constitution. The Charter would become the City’s governing document upon voter approval and
p7 filing with the California Secretary of State.
p7 As a general law city, Orange is governed primarily by state law. As a charter city, Orange would continue to be subject to the United States Constitution,
p7 the California Constitution, and state law on matters of statewide concern, but would have authority over municipal affairs.
p7 The proposed Charter would retain the City’s Council-Manager form of government and continue governance by an elected Mayor and City Council.
p7 It would establish lifetime term limits of two four-year terms for Mayor and three four-year terms for Council Members. These term limits would apply
p7 prospectively and would not count terms served before the Charter becomes effective. The Charter also provides that election procedures, district
p7 boundaries, qualifications for office, and related election matters will continue to be governed by ordinance and applicable law.
p7 The proposed Charter would permit the City to exercise the authority available to charter cities over municipal contracting and procurement procedures,
p7 to the extent permitted by law. It would establish an Open Space Reserve consisting of City-owned properties designated by the City Council and
p7 would require voter approval before property may be removed from the Reserve or used for purposes inconsistent with the Charter. The Charter also
p7 includes provisions regarding eminent domain, protection of private property rights, vacancies in office, fiscal management, balanced budgets, annual
p7 independent audits, and priorities for the use of additional recurring General Fund revenues.
p7 If approved, the Charter would supersede conflicting provisions of general law with respect to municipal affairs to the extent authorized by the California
p7 Constitution. On matters of statewide concern, state law would continue to govern. Existing ordinances, resolutions, contracts, rights, obligations, and
p7 liabilities would remain in effect unless modified in accordance with the Charter or applicable law.
p7 A “Yes” vote on Measure K approves the proposed Charter and authorizes the City of Orange to become a charter city.
p7 A “No” vote on Measure K rejects the proposed Charter, and the City of Orange would remain a general law city governed by existing state law.
