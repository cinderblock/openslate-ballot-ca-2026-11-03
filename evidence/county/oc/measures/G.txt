p2 Full Text of Measure G
p2 City of Los Alamitos
p2 ORDINANCE NO. XX
p2 AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY COUNCIL OF THE CITY OF LOS ALAMITOS, CALIFORNIA, AMENDING
p2 LOS ALAMITOS MUNICIPAL CODE SECTION 3.16.030 TO INCREASE THE CITY’S TRANSIENT
p2 OCCUPANCY TAX RATE FROM EIGHT PERCENT (8%) TO TWELVE PERCENT (12%)
p2 WHEREAS, the City of Los Alamitos (“City”) is committed to preserving the small-town character, public safety services, neighborhood quality,
p2 and fiscal stability that residents and businesses rely upon; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City continues to experience increasing operational costs associated with maintaining streets, parks, public facilities, public
p2 safety services, and other essential municipal services expected by the community; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City operates with limited local revenue sources and must carefully manage available resources to maintain essential City
p2 services and respond to community needs; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City needs dependable and local sources of revenue to fund and to support essential, necessary, and appropriate general City
p2 services provided to the public; and
p2 WHEREAS, over the last several years the State of California has gone from one financial crisis to another with no end in sight; and
p2 WHEREAS, during each of the past several years the State of California has reduced or taken funding from cities, counties, and school districts
p2 in order to fund its deficits and may continue to do so into the foreseeable future; and
p2 WHEREAS, transient occupancy tax revenues, which have been a source of locally controlled funds available to pay for essential City services,
p2 are limited; and
p2 WHEREAS, the State of California has shifted responsibility for many programs back to cities, like Los Alamitos, without the necessary funds to
p2 provide the services residents need and expect; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City’s general fund expenditures are projected to increase due to inflationary costs and unfunded mandates from the state in
p2 the near future causing the City to have challenges to maintain all levels of service and will not have capacity to fund facilities that are necessary to
p2 deliver services that the community desires; and
p2 WHEREAS, transient occupancy tax revenues, which have been a source of locally controlled funds available to pay for essential City services,
p2 are limited; and
p2 WHEREAS, transient occupancy tax revenues generated by visitors staying in local hotels provide a locally controlled source of funding that
p2 can help support general municipal services without directly increasing taxes on residents; and
p2 WHEREAS, additional locally controlled revenue would assist the City in addressing rising costs associated with public safety, infrastructure
p2 maintenance, park and facility improvements, and other community priorities identified by residents and the City Council; and
p2 WHEREAS, California Revenue and Taxation Code section 7280, et seq., authorizes the City to levy a transient occupancy tax (“TOT”) upon the
p2 privilege of occupying a hotel, as defined in Section 3.16.020 of the Los Alamitos Municipal Code, for a period of 30 days or less; and
p2 WHEREAS, many nearby jurisdictions have a TOT rate between ten percent (10%) and fifteen percent (15%). At this time, the City’s TOT rate
p2 is less than each of the following jurisdictions: Aliso Viejo, Anaheim, Brea, Buena Park, Cypress, Dana Point, Fountain Valley, Fullerton, Garden Grove,
p2 Huntington Beach, La Palma, Laguna Beach, Laguna Hills, Laguna Woods, Lake Forest, Orange, Placentia, San Clemente, San Juan Capistrano, Santa
p2 Ana, Seal Beach, Stanton, Tustin, Yorba Linda, and Orange County; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City’s current transient occupancy tax rate of eight percent (8%) is lower than many surrounding Orange County and regional
p2 jurisdictions, several of which impose rates between ten percent (10%) and fifteen percent (15%); and
p2 WHEREAS, increasing the City’s transient occupancy tax rate would help align Los Alamitos more closely with neighboring communities while
p2 allowing visitors to contribute toward the municipal services and infrastructure they utilize during their stay; and
p2 WHEREAS, the City desires to increase its TOT from the existing eight percent (8%) to twelve percent (12%); and
p2 WHEREAS, all additional revenues raised by this measure will be used to fund essential city services in Los Alamitos, and cannot be taken away
p2 by the state or used for other purposes.
p2 NOW, THEREFORE, THE PEOPE OF THE CITY OF LOS ALAMITOS, CALIFORNIA, DO ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:
p2 SECTION 1. The foregoing recitals are true and correct and are incorporated herein by reference.
p2 SECTION 2. Subject to and contingent upon the voter approval requirements set forth in Section 3 of this Ordinance, Section 3.16.030 (Tax Imposed)
p2 of Chapter 3.16 (Transient Occupancy Tax) of Title 3 (Revenue and Finance) of the Los Alamitos Municipal Code is hereby amended and restated to read
p2 as follows:
p2 3.16.030
p2 Tax imposed.
p2 For the privilege of occupancy in any hotel, each transient is subject to and shall pay a tax in the amount of twelve percent of the rent charged
p2 by the operator. Such tax constitutes a debt owned by the transient to the city which is extinguished only by payment to the operator or to the
p2 city. The transient shall pay the tax to the operator of the hotel at the time the rent is paid. If the rent is paid in installments, a proportionate
p3 share of the tax shall be paid with each installment. The unpaid tax shall be due upon the transient’s ceasing to occupy space in the hotel. If
p3 for any reason the tax due is not paid to the operator of the hotel, the tax administrator may require that such tax shall be paid directly to the
p3 tax administrator.
p3 SECTION 3. Approval by the Voters. Pursuant to California Elections Code section 9217 and Revenue and Taxation Code section 7285.9, this Ordinance
p3 and the transactions and use tax contemplated herein shall take effect only if approved by a majority of the eligible voters of the City of Los Alamitos
p3 voting at the Regular Election of November 3, 2026, and shall take effect ten (10) days after the City Council has certified the results of that election
p3 by resolution.
p3 Impartial Analysis
p3 City of Los Alamitos
p3 Measure G
p3 The City of Los Alamitos (“City”) currently imposes a transient occupancy tax (“TOT”), also generally known as a “hotel” tax, at the rate of eight percent
p3 (8%) of rent paid by guests to occupy a hotel room or similar lodging. The TOT is imposed upon occupancy of a hotel room or similar lodging within the
p3 City for a period of thirty (30) consecutive days or less. The TOT currently provides approximately $600,000.00 annually to the General Fund to pay for
p3 general City services and programs.
p3 Measure G was placed on the ballot by the Los Alamitos City Council and, if approved by a majority of Los Alamitos voters, will amend the Los Alamitos
p3 Municipal Code to increase the TOT tax rate from eight percent (8%) to twelve percent (12%) of the rent paid by guests to occupy a hotel room or similar
p3 lodging. It is anticipated that this rate increase will generate an additional $300,000.00 in new revenues.
p3 Measure G would be a “general tax”. All revenue from this tax would be deposited into the City’s General Fund and could be used for general City
p3 operations and services, including public safety, infrastructure maintenance, park and facility improvements, and other community priorities identified
p3 by residents and the City Council. Pursuant to State Law, a “general tax” requires approval by a majority of the City’s voters voting at an election.
p3 A “yes” vote on Measure G is a vote to increase the TOT tax rate from 8% to 12%.
p3 A “no” vote on Measure G is a vote to maintain the current TOT tax rate at 8%.

p4 Argument in Favor of Measure G
p4 VOTE YES ON MEASURE G, LOS ALAMITOS HOTEL TAX (TOT MEASURE)
p4 MEASURE
p4 People live in Los Alamitos because it offers an exceptional quality of life
p4 and great local services while maintaining fiscal responsibility. Measure G
p4 is a practical, locally controlled solution that will help protect essential
p4 city services without increasing taxes on residents.
p4 Measure G increases the Transient Occupancy Tax (TOT) paid by hotel,
p4 motel, and short-term rental guests from 8% to 12%. This tax is paid
p4 by visitors staying in Los Alamitos, not by local residents. Even with this
p4 adjustment, Los Alamitos’ hotel tax rate will remain competitive with
p4 neighboring cities.
p4 If approved, Measure G is expected to generate approximately $300,000
p4 annually to support community priorities, including:
p4 • Maintaining 911 emergency response and police services
p4 • Providing neighborhood police patrols and public safety programs
p4 • Maintaining parks and community facilities
p4 • Supporting youth, afterschool, and senior programs
p4 • Repairing streets, potholes, and other critical infrastructure
p4 Additionally, our city will see an increase in tourism as Olympic sporting
p4 events take place in neighboring communities. These visitors bring
p4 increased traffic and demand on Los Alamitos’ city services, including
p4 streets, emergency medical services, and police protection. However, the
p4 cost of providing those services to visitors disproportionately falls on our
p4 local taxpayers.
p4 As costs continue to rise, Measure G provides a reliable source of local
p4 funding that cannot be taken by the State and remains under local
p4 control. Every dollar generated will stay in Los Alamitos and be used to
p4 support services that directly benefit our community.
p4 This measure helps ensure that visitors contribute their fair share toward
p4 the services and infrastructure they use while visiting our city. It is a
p4 sensible approach that strengthens public safety, preserves quality of life,
p4 and invests in the future of Los Alamitos.
p4 Vote YES on Measure G.
