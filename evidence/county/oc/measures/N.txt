p2 AN ORDINANCE OF THE PEOPLE OF THE CITY OF
p2 SANTA ANA AMENDING PORTIONS OF ARTICLE VII OF CHAPTER 35
p2 OF THE SANTA ANA MUNICIPAL CODE RELATED TO
p2 A PREVIOUSLY ADOPTED TRANSACTIONS AND USE TAX
p2 ALREADY ADMINISTERED BY THE STATE BOARD OF EQUALIZATION
p2 The People of the City of Santa Ana do ordain as follows:
p2 SECTION 1. Authority. The People of the City of Santa Ana hereby adopt this ordinance amending portions of Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa
p2 Ana Municipal Code modifying a previously adopted Transactions and Use Tax in accordance with the authority granted to cities by Article XI, Section
p2 7 of the California Constitution and Section 7285.9 of the California Revenue and Taxation Code (underlined language is added and stricken language
p2 is deleted in strikethrough.)
p2 SECTION 2. Section 35-204 of Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa Ana Municipal Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
p2 Sec. 35-204. - Imposition of transactions and use tax—Rate.
p2 For the privilege of selling tangible personal property at retail, a tax is hereby imposed upon all retailers in the incorporated territory of the city
p2 at the rate of one and one half cents (1.5) of any gross receipts of the retailer from the sale of all tangible personal property sold at retail in the city
p2 on and after the operative date of this article until March 31, 2029 and then at the rate of one cent (1) until March 31, 2039.
p2 SECTION 3. Section 35-206 of Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa Ana Municipal Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
p2 Sec. 35-206. - Imposition of use tax—Use tax rate.
p2 An excise tax is hereby imposed on the storage, use, or other consumption in the city of tangible personal property purchased from any retailer
p2 on or after the operative date of this article, at the rate of one and one-half cents (1.5) of the sales price of the property subject to the tax until
p2 March 31, 2029 and then at the rate of one cent (1) of the sales price of the property subject to the tax until March 31, 2039. The sales price shall
p2 include delivery charges when such charges are subject to state sales or use tax regardless of the place to which delivery is made.
p2 SECTION 4. Section 35-213 of Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa Ana Municipal Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
p2 Sec. 35-213. – Reserved. Termination.
p2 The authority set forth in this Article VII to impose a retail transactions and use tax shall continue until March 31, 2039.
p2 SECTION 5. Section 35-217 of Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa Ana Municipal Code is hereby amended to read as follows:
p2 Sec. 35-217. – Reserved. Sunset provision.
p2 On March 31, 2029, the rate of one and one half cents (1.5) will be reduced to one cent (1) until March 31, 2039 for a total of twenty (20) years.
p2 SECTION 6. If any section, sentence, clause or phrase of this ordinance is for any reason held to be invalid or unconstitutional by a decision of a court
p2 of competent jurisdiction, such decision shall not affect the validity of the remaining provisions of this Ordinance. The People of the City of Santa Ana
p2 hereby declare that they would have adopted this Ordinance irrespective of the invalidity of any particular portion thereof and intends that the invalid
p2 portions should be severed and the balance of the Ordinance be enforced.
p2 SECTION 7. Pursuant to California Elections Code section 9222, this Ordinance must be approved by a majority of the eligible voters of the City of
p2 Santa Ana voting at the General Municipal Election of November 3, 2026.
p2 SECTION 8. This Ordinance shall become effective ten days (10) days after the City Council has certified the results of the General Municipal Election
p2 by resolutions.
p2 SECTION 9. Following the City Clerk’s certification that the citizens of Santa Ana have approved this Ordinance, the Mayor shall sign this Ordinance
p2 and the City Clerk shall cause the same to be entered in the book of original ordinances of said City; and shall cause the same, or a summary thereof,
p2 to be published as required by law.
p3 Impartial Analysis
p3 City of Santa Ana
p3 Measure N
p3 Measure N titled the “Santa Ana Streets, Public Safety, Essential City Services Measure” was placed on the ballot by a vote of the Santa Ana City
p3 Council. It seeks to indefinitely continue the existing sales tax past 2039, by removing both the sunset provision and the 2029 reduction, as originally
p3 contained in Article VII of Chapter 35 of the Santa Ana Municipal Code. In 2018, Santa Ana’s voters enacted an ordinance creating a 20-year local
p3 transactions and use tax in at a rate of one and one half (1.5) cents, until March 31, 2029 with a reduction to one (1) cent until March 31, 2039, to
p3 fund general city services. With the exception of those items that are exempt, the transactions and use tax applies to most over-the-counter sales of
p3 tangible goods and restaurant purchases.
p3 Measure N amends the previously enacted local transactions and use tax by removing the rate reduction from 1.5 cents to 1 cent scheduled to
p3 occur on April 1, 2029 and removing the sunset provision which would have ended the local transactions and use tax altogether on April 1, 2039. If
p3 the measure is approved, the local transactions and use tax would remain at 1.5 cents without an end date.
p3 If approved by more than 50% of the voters, Measure N will continue the local transactions and use tax at a rate of 1.5 cents generating
p3 approximately $84,000,000 every year for general city services. General city services may include, but is not limited to, maintaining 9-1-1 emergency
p3 response services, fixing potholes, and removing graffiti and litter. The continued revenue from the transactions and use tax would also be used to
p3 address the city’s anticipated structural deficit at the discretion of the City Council.
p3 Measure N does not amend the existing requirement for a citizen oversight committee, an annual audit by an independent certified public
p3 accountant, or an annual disclosure by the City of expenditures made from the transactions and use tax revenue. These requirements would remain
p3 in place.
p3 Measure N requires a majority of the Santa Ana voters that vote in the election of November 3, 2026 to vote in favor of the measure to become
p3 law.
p3 In summary:
p3 A “YES” vote on the measure is a vote in favor of continuing a local transactions and use tax in the City of Santa Ana at the current rate of one
p3 and one half (1.5) cents without a rate reduction or sunset provision to fund general city services until ended by the voters.
p3 A “NO” vote on the measure is a vote against removing the rate reduction from one and one half (1.5) cents to one (1) cent scheduled to occur
p3 on April 1, 2029 and against removing the sunset provision which would end the transactions and use tax on April 1, 2039.

p4 Argument in Favor of Measure N
p4 Santa Ana families deserve safe parks and playgrounds, quality streets
p4 and infrastructure, and a clean community. We deserve fast emergency
p4 response times from our police officers and firefighters, and we deserve
p4 a good quality of life for our children and seniors-WITHOUT increasing
p4 taxes on our working families.
p4 This is why we encourage you to VOTE YES on this measure to maintain
p4 Santa Ana’s existing tax levels, maintain our streets, public safety, and
p4 essential services.
p4 Passing this ballot measure would:
p4 1. Allow the city to maintain 454 miles of streets, 90 miles of storm
p4 drains, and 371 acres of parks.
p4 2. Keep every dollar raised by this ballot measure here in Santa Ana.
p4 The county, state, and federal governments cannot take it from us.
p4 3. Ensure that we have enough police officers, firefighters, and
p4 paramedics to respond to 911 calls.
p4 4. NOT increase taxes. It only maintains the tax rate approved by
p4 voters in 2018.
p4 5. Require a committee of local residents to oversee the use of these
p4 local tax dollars.
p4 Many other Orange County cities have similar provisions in place without
p4 a sunset, including Westminster, Buena Park, Garden Grove, La Habra,
p4 Placentia, Seal Beach, and Los Alamitos.
p4 Without the passage of this ballot measure, we may not have enough first
p4 responders necessary to keep our community safe, we will have to make
p4 tough decisions regarding maintaining streets and parks, and our working
p4 families, seniors, and children will not get the quality of life Santa Ana
p4 residents deserve.
p4 A YES VOTE protects our streets, parks, public safety staffing levels, and
p4 essential services.
p4 A YES VOTE will protect Santa Ana’s future.
