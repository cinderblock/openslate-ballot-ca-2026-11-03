p3 Impartial Analysis
p3 City of Costa Mesa
p3 Measure C
p3 Measure C, known as “The Costa Mesa Fair Business License Reform, Small Business Protection Measure” (referred to herein as the “Measure”) would
p3 amend Section 9-25 of Chapter I of Article 9 of the Costa Mesa Municipal Code, which was originally adopted in 1985 and has remained unchanged
p3 in the 41 years since then. This tax is charged to businesses using city services, but not to residents. Small businesses, those making $500,000 or less,
p3 would not pay any more tax than they do currently. The Measure would update the business license tax for businesses making more than $500,000
p3 annual gross receipts, up to a maximum amount of $15,000 per year for businesses earning $30 million per year or more. The existing maximum tax is
p3 $200 per year no matter how much a business earns, resulting in small businesses paying a much higher proportion of their gross receipts than large
p3 businesses in the City do. Business license tax receipts in the City are currently approximately $1 million.
p3 The updated business license tax structure will remain the same for over 72% of existing businesses. For those earning more than $500,000 annually,
p3 the rate will be fifty cents per one thousand dollars of gross receipts, as already defined in the municipal code. The Measure was placed on the ballot
p3 by the City Council.
p3 The Measure provides that the City Council may reduce the tax rate for specific types of businesses if it determines that economic conditions affecting
p3 a particular type or size business justify reducing their tax. The City Council could subsequently revise the tax rate for such businesses, but not above
p3 the maximum provided in the schedule.
p3 The Measure is anticipated to raise an additional $4.6 million dollars annually, to be placed into the general fund to support 911 emergency, fire,
p3 and police response, crime prevention, homelessness prevention, street and pothole repair, park maintenance, attracting and retaining small local
p3 businesses, and other general city services. All funds received will be locally controlled and subject to annual public spending disclosures and audit,
p3 and will help the city maintain financial stability.
p3 A “no” vote would leave the existing business license tax in place and maintain the existing $200 cap adopted in 1985.

p4 Argument in Favor of Measure C
p4 Costa Mesa has not increased the cost of a business license since 1985.
p4 Presently, the highest amount a business pays for a business license
p4 is $200 per year. That goes for the city’s large retailers, automobile
p4 dealerships, large industrial companies and international law firms.
p4 It is not reasonable to keep the business license costs the same when all
p4 of the city’s expenses have increased substantially over time. No other
p4 city in our area had the same business license costs for 41 years.
p4 Over that time, consumer costs have increased by over 200%. The City
p4 of Costa Mesa’s expenses have increased at a similar pace. The costs of
p4 our city workers, including police officers and firefighters, have increased,
p4 as has the costs to maintain and improve our streets, parks and facilities.
p4 Many of our buildings are decades old, including fire stations. This creates
p4 potential risks to our first responders.
p4 The proposed increase in the business license fee will not apply to
p4 businesses with less than $500,000 in sales. Other businesses will see
p4 a modest increase. For instance, a business with $1 million in sales will
p4 pay $500 per year for its business license or 00.05% of its sales. This is a
p4 reasonable price for the city services the business receives.
p4 If Measure C passes, the city will receive an additional $4.6 million each
p4 year. This will help the city pay for increasing costs of city services (e.g.,
p4 police and fire), and make needed improvements to our city buildings,
p4 parks, streets and Information Technology.
p4 We urge you to vote yes on Measure C so that the City of Costa Mesa can
p4 continue to provide first class service and improve our infrastructure.
