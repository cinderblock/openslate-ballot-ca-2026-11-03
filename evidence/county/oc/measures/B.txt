p2 PROJECT LIST
p2 The Board of Trustees of the Westminster School District is committed to maintaining the quality of education in our local public schools by
p2 upgrading aging classrooms and improving student safety by providing safe, secure, modern classrooms, labs and technology needed to support
p2 high quality instruction in math, science, engineering, technology, art, music and career technical education. To that end, the Board evaluated
p2 the District’s urgent and critical facility needs, including safety issues, class size reduction, basic repairs, computer and information technology in
p2 developing the types of projects to be funded by this measure as described below.
p2 This bond includes strict accountability requirements to make sure public funds are used appropriately. These requirements include public
p2 disclosure of all spending, independent annual financial and performance audits, and the formation of a committee of local citizens and business
p2 owners to ensure all spending is in line with the promises made in the measure. What’s more, by law, all funds must be used to improve our local
p2 schools and cannot be taken by Sacramento, the federal government, the County, or anyone else.
p2 In approving this Project List, the Board of Trustees determines that the District must:
p2 (i) KEEP computer systems and instructional technology up-to-date; and
p2 (ii) IMPROVE STUDENT SAFETY and campus security systems that will protect students and staff in case of an emergency;
p2 and
p2 (iii) ENSURE all money raised will stay local to support students; and
p2 (iv) REPAIR or replace deteriorating roofs and plumbing; and
p2 (v) PROVIDE the classrooms, facilities, and technology needed to support high quality instruction; and
p2 (vi) REQUIRE a transparent system of accountability, including a Project List detailing how money will be used, a citizens’
p2 oversight committee and public disclosure of how all funds are spent.
p2 The Project List includes the following types of upgrades and improvements at District schools, facilities and sites:
p2 LOCALLY CONTROLLED FUNDING FOR SCHOOL IMPROVEMENTS
p2 School Repair and Safety Projects
p2 Goals and Purposes:
p2 Most of our schools were built in the 1950s and 1960s and need to be repaired. Last year alone, there were 135 classrooms and facilities
p2 with leaky roofs. It’s time to make essential repairs and improvements so our schools can serve our community well for the decades to
p2 come.
p2 Some of our schools were built so long ago that they don’t meet modern health codes, building safety codes, or have proper access for
p2 students with disabilities. We need to fix these issues.
p2 • Upgrade restrooms that were constructed 70 years ago.
p2 • Repair or replace deteriorating roofs, plumbing, HVAC, gas lines, sewer lines, and electrical systems where needed.
p2 • Replace aging portable classrooms that are expensive to repair and maintain with modern, permanent classrooms that meet today’s academic
p2 standards.
p2 • Improve student safety and campus security, systems including security fencing, security cameras, intruder protection systems, emergency
p2 communications systems, smoke detectors, fire alarms, and sprinklers.
p2 SUPPORT HIGH QUALITY INSTRUCTION IN MATH, SCIENCE, ENGINEERING AND TECHNOLOGY
p2 Projects That Improve Student Success
p2 Goals and Purposes:
p2 If we want our kids to succeed in high school, college and careers, they must be skilled in the use of today’s technologies and have a solid
p3 background in science, math, engineering and technology. This measure will make this possible.
p3 Even if you do not have school-age children, supporting this school bond is a wise investment. Good schools improve the quality of life in
p3 our community and protect the value of our homes.
p3 • Keep computer systems and instructional technology up-to-date.
p3 • Provide the classrooms, facilities and technology needed to support high quality instruction in math, science, engineering, and
p3 technology.
p3 • Create modern, multi-use classrooms to support hands-on science instruction and learning-by-doing.
p3 • Provide modern labs, career technology and equipment so students are prepared for high school, college and in-demand careers in
p3 fields like health sciences, engineering, technology, and skilled trades.
p3 • Provide the classrooms, facilities and technology needed to support high quality instruction in math, science, engineering, and technology.

p4 FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY
p4 THIS MEASURE REQUIRES A CLEAR SYSTEM OF ACCOUNTABILITY, INCLUDING A PROJECT LIST DETAILING HOW THE MONEY WILL BE
p4 USED, A CITIZENS’ OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE, AND INDEPENDENT AUDITS TO ENSURE MONEY IS SPENT PROPERLY.
p4 In accordance with Education Code Section 15272, the Board of Trustees will appoint a citizens’ oversight committee and conduct annual
p4 independent audits to ensure that funds are spent only on District projects and for no other purpose. The expenditure of bond money on these
p4 projects is subject to stringent financial accountability requirements. By law, performance and financial audits will be performed annually, and all bond
p4 expenditures will be monitored by an independent citizens’ oversight committee to ensure that funds are spent as promised and specified. The citizens’
p4 oversight committee must include, among others, representation of a bona fide taxpayers association, a business organization and a senior citizens
p4 organization. No District employees or vendors are allowed to serve on the citizens’ oversight committee.
p4 No Administrator Salaries: Proceeds from the sale of the bonds authorized by this proposition shall be used only for the acquisition, construction,
p4 reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, and not for any other
p4 purpose, including teacher and school administrator salaries and other operating expenses.
p4 Tax Rate Statement
p4 Westminster School District
p4 Measure B
p4 An election will be held in the Westminster School District (the “District”) on November 3, 2026 to authorize the sale of up to $128,000,000
p4 in general obligation bonds. If such bonds are authorized and sold, principal and interest on the bonds will be payable only from the proceeds of ad
p4 valorem tax levies made upon the taxable property in the District. The following information is submitted in compliance with Section 9401 of the
p4 Elections Code of the State of California. Such information is based upon the best estimates and projections presently available from official sources,
p4 upon experience within the District, and other demonstrable factors.
p4 The following information is provided pursuant to Elections Code Section 9401:
p4 1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond
p4 debt service, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $30.00 per $100,000 (3.0¢ per $100)
p4 of assessed valuation. It is currently expected that the tax will be collected until fiscal year 2060-61.
p4 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue, for each year that bonds are outstanding,
p4 based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of filing this statement, is $30.00 per $100,000 (3.0¢ per $100) of assessed
p4 valuation, which is projected to be the same in every fiscal year the bonds remain outstanding.
p4 3. The best estimate of total debt service, including principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold
p4 will be approximately $276 million.
p4 Voters should note that these estimates are based on projections derived from information obtained from official sources, and are based on the
p4 assessed value (not market value) of taxable property on the County’s official tax rolls. Property owners should consult their own property tax bills and
p4 tax advisors to determine their property’s assessed value and any applicable tax exemptions.
p4 The attention of all voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based upon projections and estimates only, which amounts
p4 are not maximum amounts and durations and are not binding upon the District. The actual debt service, tax rates and the years in which they will apply
p4 may vary depending on the timing of bond sales, the par amount of bonds sold at each sale and actual increases in assessed valuations. The timing
p4 of the bond sales and the amount of bonds sold at any given time will be determined by the District based on the need for project funds and other
p4 considerations. Actual assessed valuations will depend upon the amount and value of taxable property within the District as determined by the County
p4 Assessor in the annual assessment and the equalization process.

p5 Impartial Analysis
p5 Westminster School District
p5 Measure B
p5 This measure was placed on the ballot by the governing board (“Board”) of the Westminster School District (“District”). This measure, if approved
p5 by 55 percent of the votes cast on the measure, will authorize the District to issue and sell up to $128,000,000 in general obligation bonds. The sale of
p5 these bonds by the District represents a debt of the District.
p5 Voter approval of this measure will also authorize an annual tax to be levied on taxable property in the District to generate revenue to pay
p5 principal and interest on the bonds. The District’s stated best estimate in its tax rate statement of the average annual tax rate required to fund the bonds
p5 is $30 per $100,000 of assessed value. The final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is fiscal year 2060-61. The best estimate of the
p5 total debt service, including principal and interest, required to be repaid if all bonds are issued and sold is $276,000,000. The interest rate and maturity
p5 date on any bond shall not exceed the maximums allowed by law.
p5 Proceeds from the sale of bonds authorized by this measure may only be used by the District for the construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation
p5 or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, or the acquisition or lease of interests in real property
p5 for school facilities in the District. A complete list of the projects and allowed expenditures, which bonds proceeds may be spent on, is included in the
p5 full text of the measure. The Board has certified that it has evaluated safety, class size, and information technology needs in developing the project list
p5 for this bond measure.
p5 The California Constitution provides that proceeds of school district bond measures cannot be used for teacher or administrator salaries or
p5 other operating expenses and requires independent annual performance and financial audits. State law requires the establishment of an independent
p5 citizens’ oversight committee for ensuring that bond proceeds are expended as specified in the measure and as provided by law.
p5 A “YES” vote is a vote in favor of authorizing the District to issue and sell up to $128,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p5 A “NO” vote is a vote against authorizing the District to issue and sell up to $128,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
