p2 Full Text of Measure E
p2 City of Laguna Beach
p2 AN ORDINANCE OF THE CITY OF LAGUNA BEACH, CALIFORNIA, ADDING A NEW SECTION 2.04.080 TO CHAPTER 2.04 TO
p2 TITLE 2 OF THE LAGUNA BEACH MUNICIPAL CODE RELATING TO TERM LIMITS, SUBJECT TO APPROVAL OF A MAJORITY OF THE
p2 ELECTORS VOTING ON THE MEASURE.
p2 THE PEOPLE OF THE CITY OF LAGUNA BEACH DO ORDAIN AS FOLLOWS:
p2 Section 1. Amendment of the Laguna Beach Municipal Code.
p2 Pursuant to California Government Code section 36502(b) and subject to the approval of a majority of the voters of the City of Laguna Beach, the
p2 Laguna Beach Municipal Code is hereby amended to add a new Section 2.04.080, Term Limits, to Chapter 2.04, City Council, to Title 2, Administrative
p2 and Personnel, to read as follows:
p2 “§ 2.04.080. Term Limits.
p2 (A) No person shall be eligible to serve as a member of the City Council for more than two consecutive four-year terms following the adoption
p2 of this ordinance commencing with the, and including those persons elected to serve as a member of the City Council in the November 3, 2026
p2 election. Notwithstanding the foregoing, any person who is appointed by the City Council or elected to fill a vacant office of a member of the
p2 City Council for the balance of a four-year term, but serves less than two years in that office, shall be eligible for nomination and election for
p2 two consecutive four-year terms thereafter.
p2 (B) Any person who shall be ineligible for election, appointment, or further services as a member of the City Council due to the limitation set
p2 forth in subsection A of this section, shall again be eligible for election or appointment as a member of the City Council, and a new period of
p2 consecutive years of service shall commence, provided that at least two years has or will have elapsed between the most recent date of such
p2 person’s prior service as a member of the City Council and the effective date of such person’s most recent election or appointment to such
p2 office.
p2 (C) The phrase “two years” as used in subsection B of this section shall mean the period between the date of the declaration of the results
p2 of any general municipal election held in November of an even numbered year and the date of the declaration of the results of any general
p2 municipal election held in November of any immediately preceding or immediately succeeding even-numbered year, even though such period
p2 may be more or less than an actual period of two years.”
p2 Section 2. Voter Approval, Prospective Application, and Effective Date
p2 Except as provided in section 3 below, this ordinance shall take effect if approved by a majority of the eligible voters of the City of Laguna Beach voting
p2 on the measure in the general municipal election held on Tuesday, November 3, 2026, and shall only apply prospectively beginning with the terms of
p2 members of the City Council that start after the November 3, 2026 election. If so approved, this ordinance becomes effective 10 days after the City
p2 Council declares and certifies the results of the election by resolution.
p2 Section 3. Severability
p2 If any provision of this ordinance or the application thereof to any person or circumstance is held invalid, such invalidity shall not affect other provisions
p2 or applications of the ordinance which can be given effect without the invalid provision or application, and to this end the provisions of this ordinance
p2 are severable. The People hereby declare that it would have adopted this ordinance irrespective of the invalidity of any particular portion thereof.
p2 Section 4. Publication
p2 The City Clerk is hereby directed to publish this ordinance at least once, within 15 days after the election results are certified, in a newspaper of general
p2 circulation published and circulated in the City of Laguna Beach.
p2 Section 5. Attestation of Ordinance
p2 The Mayor is hereby authorized to attest to the adoption of this Ordinance by the People voting thereon on November 3, 2026, by signing where
p2 indicated below.
p3 Impartial Analysis
p3 City of Laguna Beach
p3 Measure E
p3 The City of Laguna Beach is governed by a five-member City Council whose members are elected by Laguna Beach voters to four-year terms
p3 of office. Currently, the City of Laguna Beach does not have term limits on elected City Council office.
p3 Measure E was placed on the ballot by a citizens’ initiative petition. On December 9, 2025, the City Council voted to place Measure E on the
p3 November 3, 2026 General Municipal Election, in compliance with the California Elections Code.
p3 If approved by a majority of voters (50% +1), Measure E would add Section 2.04.080 to the Laguna Beach Municipal Code to establish term
p3 limits on City Council service. Measure E would provide that no person may serve more than two consecutive four-year terms as a Council Member. In
p3 the event a person is appointed or elected to fill a vacant City Council office for less than two years of a four-year term, that individual will be eligible
p3 to serve two consecutive four-year terms thereafter.
p3 Any person whose Council Member service has reached the term limits described above is not eligible to serve again as a Council Member,
p3 whether by appointment or election, for two years following the conclusion of that person’s last term of office as a Council Member. The term “two
p3 years” is defined, for purposes of re-eligibility for office, to mean the period between the date of the declaration of the results of any general municipal
p3 election held in November of an even-numbered year and the date of declaration of the results of any general municipal election in November of any
p3 immediately preceding or immediately succeeding even-numbered year. After this two-year period, a person may serve another two consecutive four-
p3 year City Council terms, as described above.
p3 Measure E will only apply to City Council terms starting as of the November 3, 2026 General Municipal Election. State law does not allow local
p3 term limits to apply retroactively to prior terms. Therefore, any four-year term for any City Council office elected at the November 5, 2024 General
p3 Municipal Election or at any prior election, or any prior-appointed or prior-elected partial City Council terms, will not count toward these term limits.
p3 A “yes” vote on Measure E will approve the establishment of City Council term limits as described above.
p3 A “no” vote on Measure E will not approve the establishment of City Council term limits as described above and the City of Laguna Beach will
p3 continue not to have City Council term limits.
p3 The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure E. If you desire a copy of the Measure, please call the Elections Official’s office at
p3 (949) 497-0309 and a copy will be mailed at no cost to you.
p3 /s/ Megan Garibaldi, City Attorney
p4 Argument in Favor of Measure E
p4 82% of OC cities have term limits for their City Councils. As it turns 100
p4 years old, the City of Laguna Beach is one of the few that doesn’t.
p4 The power of incumbency is immense. It often results in entrenched
p4 politicians who become less responsive to voters and more beholden to
p4 special interests.
p4 Running against an incumbent is a big deterrent for newcomers, as
p4 incumbents have the advantage of inside information, free media
p4 exposure, staff support, name recognition, and an established political
p4 base with donors in place. The pattern is indisputable; new comers have
p4 to spend significantly more than incumbents in order to win a seat. Term
p4 limits helps level the playing field.
p4 Term limits ensure that no one person can amass enough political power
p4 to effectively control the city. Term limits also enliven our democracy by
p4 bringing more people with fresh new ideas onto council.
p4 Term Limits would add a common sense restriction on who can stand for
p4 election to council, similar to requirements of minimum age, citizenship,
p4 and city residence.
p4 If passed, starting after the 2026 general election, council members
p4 will be limited to two sequential four-year council terms. They would be
p4 eligible to run again after a two-year hiatus. Councilpersons who term-
p4 out needn’t disappear; they can continue contributing to civic life through
p4 advisory roles, appointment to commissions or boards, or other forms of
p4 public service before becoming eligible to run again for council.
p4 With Laguna’s staggered election cycle, there will always be at least two
p4 members remaining on the Council to help ensure there’s a continuity of
p4 experience and governance.
p4 We urge you to vote YES for this common sense tool to reduce the
p4 power of special interests, broaden the pool of candidates, and
p4 revitalize Laguna’s governance.

p4 Treasurer, Nimblegov PAC

p4 Rebuttal to Argument in Favor of Measure E
p4 Laguna Beach works. Our coastline is protected, our neighborhoods are
p4 walkable, and our community events still feel like community events.
p4 People choose to live here because this city is governed well and held
p4 accountable. Laguna voters turn out at over 80%, one of the highest rates
p4 in Orange County.
p4 Now a small group wants to change the rules, not because the system
p4 is broken, but because they keep losing under it. The same people who
p4 pushed a rejected ballot measure in 2022 and failed to win a council seat
p4 in 2024 are back, this time asking voters to surrender the right to reelect
p4 the leaders they trust.
p4 The record does not support their case. Since 1992, 76% of Laguna Beach
p4 council members served two four-year terms or less. Only two have ever
p4 served four consecutive terms. Today, three of five council members are
p4 serving their first terms. In our last election, a fourth-term incumbent won
p4 more votes than any other candidate on the ballot, because the voters
p4 chose him.
p4 A UC San Diego study of all 50 states found that when experienced
p4 officials are forced out by a schedule, power does not shift to the public.
p4 It shifts to lobbyists, unelected staff, and special interests who never have
p4 to face a ballot.
p4 Elections are the real accountability tool. Laguna uses them exceptionally
p4 well.
p4 What we have is working. Do not let a small group repeatedly rejected by
p4 voters rewrite the rules for the rest of us.

p4 s/ Rebecca Visconti

p4 s/ Joy Dittberner

p5 Argument Against Measure E
p5 Does Laguna need term limits? No. Will we be better off with them? No!
p5 Is there a need? The record shows Laguna voters are among the most
p5 civically engaged in Orange County, with turnout typically over 80%.
p5 Council members win elections because they’re good and constituents
p5 trust them— not because the system is broken. Since 1992, 76% of city
p5 council members served two terms or fewer; four served three consecutive
p5 terms, and just two served four. In our last election, a councilmember won
p5 his fourth term with more votes than any other candidate. Laguna Beach
p5 city council has 3 fresh faces serving their first term, two elected in 2022
p5 and one in 2024. In the last four elections, four incumbents were voted
p5 out, losing their seats to new council members. The system already turns
p5 over.
p5 Will Laguna be better off with limits? No! They don’t create a
p5 “wider range” of candidates — they force out experienced officials on
p5 an arbitrary clock. That hands power to unelected staff and insiders
p5 while newly elected members are still learning the ropes. Laguna faces
p5 complex, long-term issues that benefit from experienced leadership.
p5 Equating tenure with corruption insults the officials and the voters who
p5 chose them.
p5 Term limits don’t add accountability — elections are the real accountability
p5 mechanism. Voters should be able to choose who they want without
p5 artificial barriers. Tenure doesn’t guarantee competence, but term limits
p5 guarantee the opposite: a less experienced council plus voters stripped of
p5 the right to reelect leaders they still trust.
p5 Proponents of term limits ask you to trade your right to vote for candidates
p5 you trust. Don’t let them. This same group sponsored a losing 2022 ballot
p5 measure. They then lost a 2024 council bid. Don’t surrender your right to
p5 choose because they lost under the current system.
p5 Protect your right to vote. No on term limits.
p5 s/ Chris Quilter
p5 s/ Rebecca Visconti
p5 s/ Joe Hanauer
p5 s/ Joy Dittberner

p5 Rebuttal to Argument Against Measure E
p5 Laguna Beach is a unique city, but our system of government is no
p5 different from that of any other city. While we face complex challenges,
p5 keeping councilmembers in office for many years is not the solution. Over
p5 time, some long-serving officials run out of new ideas while others lose
p5 touch with the residents they were elected to represent.
p5 We currently have one councilmember who will have served 16 years by
p5 the end of their term and another running for their 3rd term. Despite
p5 significant resident opposition, they continue to strongly advocate for the
p5 purchase of Laguna Canyon Road. Are they still listening to the people
p5 they represent? Did the decision to remove the mature trees on Forest
p5 Avenue reflect the wishes of the community?
p5 Opponents of term limits argue that we would lose valuable experience
p5 on the City Council. That argument overlooks an important fact:
p5 councilmembers can continue serving Laguna in many meaningful ways
p5 after completing two four-year terms and they may run again after a two-
p5 year break. In addition, Laguna’s staggered election cycle ensures that
p5 experienced councilmembers remain on the dais to provide continuity
p5 and institutional knowledge. Our initiative preserves the opportunity for
p5 respected former councilmembers to return while giving qualified new
p5 candidates a fair chance to serve.
p5 Incumbency is one of the greatest advantages any elected official has in
p5 seeking re-election. This initiative creates a thoughtful balance between
p5 preserving experience and opening the door to new leadership, fresh
p5 ideas, and greater accountability.
p5 Please vote YES for term limits.
