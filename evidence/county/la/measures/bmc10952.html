CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE L

Measure L would enact an ordinance establishing a transient occupancy tax in the City of Lynwood. The City does not currently impose such a tax. Measure L would impose a tax equal to 12% of the rent charged for occupying a hotel, motel, inn, or similar lodging in the City for 30 days or less. Lodging guests would pay the tax. This is not a property tax or sales tax on Lynwood residents.

Lodging operators would be required to collect the tax from visitors and remit it to the City. Online travel companies, booking platforms, and similar intermediaries would also be required to collect and remit the tax on taxable amounts they receive, including booking or service fees, and would be jointly responsible with lodging operators for compliance.

The ordinance would exempt stays provided through emergency-shelter or homelessness-services programs and stays by Lynwood residents temporarily displaced from their homes by fire, flood, or another emergency. Occupancies lasting more than 30 days under a written rental agreement would not be taxed.

Because Measure L is a general tax, all proceeds would be deposited into the City's General Fund and could be used for any lawful general municipal purpose. The ballot question identifies services that this revenue could fund, such as 911 emergency response, crime prevention, homelessness response, parks and public areas, traffic safety, street and pothole repair, and youth and senior programs. The City estimates the measure would generate approximately $751,315 annually. Actual revenue would depend on lodging activity and taxable room charges.

Measure L would require the revenue to be included in the City's annual independent financial audit. The Finance Director would provide an annual public report to the City Council identifying revenue collected and services supported, and the City would post the audit and report on its website.

The tax rate could not exceed 12% without further voter approval. The tax would continue until ended by voters. If approved, the ordinance would take effect 10 days after the City Council declares the election results, and the tax would become operative on the first day of the first calendar quarter beginning at least 60 days later.

A "yes" vote approves the ordinance and establishes the 12% transient occupancy tax.

A "no" vote rejects the ordinance, and the City continues without a transient occupancy tax.

The measure requires approval by a majority of the votes cast.
