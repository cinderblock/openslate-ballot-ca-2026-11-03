Shall the measure permitting the City of Bell Gardens to sell the Bell Gardens Municipal Water System to the Golden State Water Company be adopted? Supporters: None submitted Opponents: None submitted.

CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE W

The Bell Gardens City Council has placed Measure W on the November 2026 ballot to ask whether voters approve authorizing the City Council to sell the City’s municipal water system to the Golden State Water Company (“Golden State”) for $41 million.

BACKGROUND: The City of Bell Gardens (“City”) owns and operates a municipal water system that provides water service to approximately one-third of the City’s residents. The other two-thirds of residents receive water service from Golden State. In late 2025, the City invited other water providers to submit proposals to purchase its water system. It received two proposals, one of which was from Golden State.

Under state law, before any city may sell its water system, it must hold a noticed public hearing and then receive approval from the City’s voters. On June 8, 2026, the Bell Gardens City Council held the required hearing and voted to ask residents’ permission to sell its water system to Golden State. The election on November 3, 2026, is the voters’ opportunity to give or withhold that permission.

SUMMARY OF MEASURE: Measure W permits, but does not require, the City to sell its water system to Golden State. Under the terms of this sale, Golden State would obtain the City’s entire water system, including all of its infrastructure and water rights, with the result being that Golden State would be the water utility for all City residents rather than the two-thirds it currently serves. In exchange, Golden State would pay the City $41 million, which the City may use to fund any public purpose or service.

The sale terms further require Golden State to keep water rates at their current levels until January 1, 2028. After that date, any rate increase that Golden State seeks to impose must be reviewed and approved by the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC).

Since the CPUC regulates Golden State’s activities, its role will begin even earlier. If voters approve Measure W, the next step will be for the CPUC to review the Measure, and either approve the sale, deny it, or approve it subject to conditions. If the CPUC approves the sale on conditions that change its benefits to the City, the Bell Gardens City Council would then decide whether to accept those changes or cancel the transaction.

To pass, Measure W requires an affirmative vote of a simple majority (50% + 1) of the electorate.

VOTE: A YES vote in support of Measure W would permit the City to sell its water system to Golden State. A NO vote against Measure W would prohibit the City from selling its water system unless and until the voters approve a new and different proposal.

STEPHANIE VASQUEZ
City Attorney
