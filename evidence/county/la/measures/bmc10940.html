Modernization/No Tax Rate Increase Measure. To maintain city services, such as: Fire protection, paramedic, 911 emergency response, crime prevention and keeping streets, infrastructure, and parks safe and well-maintained shall City of Alhambra's measure modernizing the hotel/motel transient occupancy tax be adopted at the current 12% rate for stays up to 30 days to include short-term lodging, providing an additional $134,000 annually for city services until ended by voters, with all money locally controlled? Supporters: Alhambra Firefighters Association; Alhambra Police Officers Association Opponents: None submitted.

CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE HT

CITY OF ALHAMBRA

UPDATED TRANSIENT OCCUPANCY TAX ("TOT")

On July 27, 2026, the City Council unanimously authorized placing Measure HT on the ballot for consideration by the electorate at the November 3, 2026, election. If approved by a majority of the City of Alhambra ("City") voters, Measure HT would authorize two sets of amendments to the City's transient occupancy tax ("TOT"), as a general tax ("Proposed Tax").

The City's Existing TOT ordinance, codified at Alhambra Municipal Code Chapter 5.70 ("Existing TOT"), imposes a tax on guests of hotels and motels ("lodging") at the rate of 12% of the rent charged to the guest by the lodging operator. Lodging guests are referred to as "transients," and the Existing TOT: (a) defines "transients" as people with a right to occupy a lodging for 21 consecutive calendar days or less, and (b) separately defines "hotels" and "motels" in a manner that is limited to lodging facilities that either have six or more rooms (motel) or provide additional services such as restaurants and meeting rooms (hotel).

The Proposed Tax would amend the definition of "hotel" to include "motel" as well as any facility that provides temporary lodging to people. Thus, the Proposed Tax would not require a "hotel" to have a minimum number of rooms, or provide additional services, and "hotel" would include any short-term rental of a residential dwelling to a person that meets the definition of "transient."

The Proposed Tax would amend the definition of "transient" to include any person with a right to occupy a hotel for 30 consecutive calendar days or less (as opposed to 21 days under the Existing TOT). This is consistent with state law that authorizes the City to impose a TOT on a person occupying lodging for up to 30 days. (Revenue and Taxation Code Section 7280.)

The Proposed Tax would not amend the existing 12% TOT rate. However, the existing TOT rate will be applied to the newly defined "transients" occupying "hotels" to generate additional revenue of approximately $134,000 per year. The TOT is a general tax because the City may use the revenue to fund any lawful City program or service. The Proposed Tax would remain in effect until ended by a majority of City voters at a subsequent election.

A "yes" vote on Measure HT is a vote in favor of the Proposed Tax, which would amend the definitions of "transient" and "hotel" under the Existing TOT.

A "no" vote on Measure HT is a vote against the Proposed Tax, which would not amend the definitions of "transient" and "hotel" under the Existing TOT.
