SOUTH GATE FISCAL EMERGENCY STREET MAINTENANCE, YOUTH PROGRAMS, PARKS FUNDING PROTECTION MEASURE. Shall an ordinance preventing cuts to City of South Gate services including maintaining streets and sidewalks; maintaining school crossing guards, parks/recreation programs, police 911 and support services, and other General Fund uses by establishing an 8% utility user tax, exempting low income seniors, annually providing approximately $10,400,000 until ended by voters; requiring independent audits, public disclosure of all spending, local control, to benefit South Gate residents, be adopted? Supporters: Gil Hurtado, Councilmember; Hector De La Torre, Former Councilmember; South Gate POA; South Gate MEA; South Gate AYSO Opponents: California Taxpayers Association; Los Angeles County Taxpayers Association.

CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE SG

CITY OF SOUTH GATE

This measure was placed on the ballot by the City Council of the City of South Gate. If approved by more than half of the votes cast on the measure, it would adopt a new Utility Users Tax ordinance adding Chapter 2.79 to Title 2 of the South Gate Municipal Code.

The City does not currently impose a utility users tax. The measure would impose an 8% tax on the charges for telecommunications services, including mobile and VoIP; video services, including cable and streaming video; electricity; gas; and water used within the City. The tax would apply to households and businesses, and the taxable charges include franchise fees and regulatory surcharges that appear on the bill. The tax would be collected from service users by utility service suppliers as part of their regular billing and remitted to the City monthly.

The Utility Users Tax is a general tax. Revenue would be deposited into the City's General Fund and may be used for services such as public safety, street maintenance, park recreation, youth programs, and other lawful purposes. City staff estimate the tax would generate approximately $10,400,000 annually.

The measure exempts residents who are 65 years of age or older, reside at the service address as their primary residence, and whose combined household gross income does not exceed the threshold established by the Tax Administrator based on California Public Utilities Commission low-income assistance standards. Qualifying residents must file an application with the Tax Administrator, and meet annual eligibility requirements. The measure also exempts any person or service from the tax if its imposition would violate federal or state law.

The City Manager, or designee, serves as Tax Administrator responsible for enforcement, rulemaking, audits of those required to collect or remit the tax, and collection. The measure provides for deficiency determinations, penalties for late payment, refund procedures, and administrative hearings with appeal rights to the City Council.

The tax would have no expiration date. The City Council may amend the ordinance without a vote of the people to reduce the tax, except that voter approval is required for any amendment that would increase the tax.

Collection would begin after the advance notice to utility service suppliers that state law requires. Because the Utility Users Tax is a general tax, it requires approval by more than half of the votes cast on the measure at the November 3, 2026, General Municipal Election.

A "YES" vote means you are in favor of adopting an 8% utility users tax on telecommunications, video, electricity, gas, and water services to fund general City services.

A "NO" vote means you are against adopting the utility users tax.
