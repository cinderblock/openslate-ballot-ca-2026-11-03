Occupation: Research Scientist

Education and Qualifications:

Over my many years in Palo Alto, I've often asked people why they moved here. Practically everyone gives the same answer: our excellent public schools. Even as an East Bay high school student, I admired the reputation of Paly and Gunn and thought that if I ever had children, I would want them to grow up here.

As luck would have it, I came to Stanford, where I earned four degrees in mathematics and electrical engineering, culminating in a PhD in electrical engineering. Along the way, a Fulbright scholarship took me to Germany, where I met my wife, Katja. I stayed in Palo Alto, built my career here, and when it came time to raise our family, Palo Alto was the obvious choice.

I am the son of Taiwanese immigrants, while Katja brings French and German heritage to our family. We value Palo Alto's remarkable mix of backgrounds, cultures, and perspectives. Our three children attended Ohlone, JLS Connections, and Paly, and we experienced firsthand much of what makes this district extraordinary.

As an engineer, inventor, and co-founder of Shazam, I built my career turning ambitious ideas into systems that actually work. That requires hard data, deep research, creative collaboration, persistence, and the humility to listen. I want to bring that same grounded problem-solving to our School Board.

Our district has been through a turbulent period. Too often, families have felt treated as adversaries, educators have felt unheard, academic opportunities have been reduced, and disagreements have escalated into costly legal battles. We need a genuine cultural reset based on trust, transparency, empathy, and collaboration.

Academic excellence and equity should reinforce each other. Every student deserves an education that recognizes individual strengths, needs, interests, and aspirations. We should provide strong foundations, appropriate support, and meaningful opportunities for every student to grow and thrive. Raising the floor should never require lowering the ceiling.

Our policies should follow credible evidence and honest measures of student learning—not ideology or educational fashion. We must also be responsible stewards of public funds: prioritizing classrooms and competitive educator pay, scrutinizing spending, and replacing costly legal confrontation with earlier, collaborative problem-solving.

I don't pretend to have every answer. I will listen with humility, ask hard questions, follow the evidence, and work to restore the excellence, trust, and partnership our community expects from its schools.

I hope to earn your vote!
