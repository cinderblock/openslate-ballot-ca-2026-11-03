CITY ATTORNEY'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE G

This measure would amend Sunnyvale City Charter Section 1309 to allow the City to use alternative methods to select contractors for public works contracts, and authorize the City Council to establish, by ordinance, any regulations for the award, use, evaluation, and approval of these contracts.

The Council placed this measure on the ballot on recommendation by a Charter Review Committee of appointed Sunnyvale residents.

Currently, Section 1309 of the Sunnyvale City Charter requires the City to use a traditional system for awarding public works contracts, known as "design-bid-build." This method requires separate contract awards for the design and construction phases of the project. Unless there is a recognized exemption, such as emergency work, the City first contracts with architects or engineers to design the project. After the project design is completed, the City then advertises the completed design for construction bids, and finally awards the construction contract to the lowest "responsive and responsible" bidder. This competitive bidding process is based on the bid price alone and does not permit the City to consider the construction contractor's qualifications, technical experience, or other factors, so long as the contractor meets the minimum requirements.

The Sunnyvale Charter does not allow any other project delivery methods for procuring and contracting work for public works projects, even when those methods are permitted by state law. For example, two alternatives are "design-build" and "construction manager-at-risk." Under a design-build approach, the design and construction phases are combined into a single contract, and a single entity is responsible for both the design and construction of a public works project. Further, the City would select a contractor through a qualifications-based process that can take into account various factors other than the lowest bid. Under a construction manager-at-risk approach, the City would hire a construction manager to perform both pre-construction and construction management services within a guaranteed maximum price (GMP), and the construction manager would be responsible for any construction costs that exceed the GMP.

This measure would authorize the City Council to choose alternative options for the delivery of public works construction projects so long as those options are allowed by California law. The measure would authorize the Council to adopt ordinances regulating the award, use, evaluation, and approval of public works contracts using such alternative methods. In addition, this measure would make clarifying changes to some existing language in Section 1309 without altering the meaning.

A "YES" vote amends the Charter so that the Council can authorize the use of alternative project delivery methods for public works contracts and make clarifying changes to the wording of Charter Section 1309.

A "NO" vote would not add any options to use alternative project delivery methods for public works contracts and would not change the wording of Charter Section 1309.

This amendment will become effective if a majority of those voting on the measure vote "yes."

§ 1309. Contracts on Public Works.

(a) Unless an alternative project delivery method is authorized as provided in this section, a Every project involving an expenditure of more than an amount to be determined from time to time by ordinance of the City Council, for the construction or improvement (excluding maintenance and repair) of public buildings, works, streets, drains, sewers, utilities, parks and playgrounds, shall be let to the lowest responsive and responsible bidder after notice of publication in the official newspaper by one or more insertions, the first of which shall be at least ten days before the time for opening bids.

The City Council may reject any and all bids presented and may readvertise in its discretion.

The City Council, without advertising for bids, or after rejecting bids, or if no bids are received, may declare and determine that, in its opinion, the work in question may be performed better or more economically by the City with its own employees, and after adoption of a resolution to this effect by at least four affirmative votes it may proceed to have said work done in the manner stated, without further observance of the provisions of this section. Such contracts likewise may be let without advertising for bids, if such work shall be deemed by the City Council to be of urgent necessity for the preservation of life, health or property, shall be authorized by motion passed by at least four affirmative votes and containing a declaration of the facts constituting such urgency. The City Council may make urgency findings and authorize by at least four affirmative votes the letting without advertising for bids of contracts that are urgently necessary for the preservation of life, health, or property.

(b) No advertising for bids or awarding of a contract based thereon shall beon bids is required when the improvement or work is to be furnished or performed by a public utility subject to the jurisdiction of the California Public Utilities Commission.

(c) The use of best value alternative project delivery methods including, but not limited to, progressive design-build, construction manager at risk, fixed-price design-build, and design-build-operate contracts is authorized for all public works projects. ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE G

Vote YES to Modernize Sunnyvale's Public Works Contracting.

Sunnyvale's rules for building public infrastructure have not kept pace with modern times. Under current Charter constraints, the City is required to use an outdated "lowest-bid" method that separates project design from construction. This rigid requirement often results in communication gaps, costly mid-project change orders, and frustrating construction delays.

This measure updates Charter Section 1309 to grant Sunnyvale access to the same flexible, modern project delivery methods already authorized by California State Law and successfully used by nearby cities and school districts.

Why a YES Vote Makes Sense:

  • Saves Taxpayer Money: By allowing modern delivery models like "Design-Build," the City can combine design and construction into a single, unified contract. This means the team that designs the project builds it, significantly reducing errors, change orders, and budget overruns.
  • Speeds Up Projects: Combining design and construction means critical civic improvements and infrastructure projects get completed much faster, minimizing disruption to our community.
  • Prioritizes Quality and Value: Sunnyvale would be allowed to consider a contractor's specialized qualifications, proven track record, and technical expertise—not just the lowest bid—ensuring high-quality infrastructure built to last.

Strict Safeguards Remain Intact

This measure does not eliminate competitive bidding. Traditional lowest-bid tracking will still be used for everyday maintenance projects. For complex projects, any alternative method must be explicitly authorized by the City Council on a case-by-case basis under strict, transparent ordinances.

Following an intensive, multi-year review, the independent, citizen-led Charter Review Committee and your City Council recommended this upgrade. Let's build a more efficient, cost-effective Sunnyvale.

Vote YES for higher quality infrastructure at lower cost.
