COUNTY COUNSEL'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE R

A school district may levy a qualified special tax upon approval by two-thirds of the votes cast on a measure pursuant to article XIIIA, section 4 of the California Constitution and California Government Code section 50079. The Board of Trustees (Board) of the Orchard School District (District) has placed on the ballot a measure to levy a parcel tax of 4 cents per square foot per parcel per year on property within the District. The Measure states that the tax would be capped annually at $89 per residential parcel and $7,500 per non-residential parcel, and would commence on July 1, 2027 and continue for eight (8) years.

State law requires that such measures state the specific purposes for which tax proceeds will be used and spend the proceeds only for these purposes. The stated purposes of the tax proposed by Measure R include, but are not limited to:

  • Recruit, train, and retain highly qualified teacher and instructional staff;
  • Maintain and protect strong core academic programs;
  • Strengthen reading and writing skills for all students while providing targeted individual support;
  • Retain student mental health counselors, social workers, and healthcare professionals;
  • Create, expand, enhance, and preserve Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEAM) education, including artificial intelligence, computer science and coding, and robotics; and
  • Provide current instructional materials, technology, spaces, equipment, and resources.

According to the Measure, the proposed tax would not apply to property that is otherwise exempt from or upon which no property taxes are levied that year. The Measure provides that multiple parcels that are (1) contiguous; (2) used solely as owner-occupied, single-family residences; and (3) held under identical ownership may, upon application by the owners by June 15 of any year, be treated as a single parcel for purposes of the tax.

The Measure also states that an owner of a parcel used solely for owner-occupied, single-family residential purposes who is (1) at least 65 years old by June 30 of the prior fiscal year; (2) receiving Supplemental Security Income for a disability, regardless of age; or (3) receiving Social Security Disability Insurance benefits, regardless of age, and whose yearly income does not exceed 250 percent of the 2012 federal poverty guidelines issued by the United States Department of Health and Human Services, may obtain an exemption upon application of the owners by June 15 of any year.

Pursuant to State law, the Measure provides accountability measures for the tax proceeds, including (1) stating that the proceeds shall be applied only to the specific purposes identified in the Measure; (2) creating a separate account into which the proceeds must be deposited; and (3) providing an annual report to the Board showing the amount of funds collected and expended and the status of any projects or programs required or authorized to be funded by the tax.

A "yes" vote is a vote to levy a parcel tax of 4 cents per square foot per parcel per year on property within the District, capped annually, as stated in the Measure, at $89 per residential parcel and $7,500 per non-residential parcel, for eight years beginning July 1, 2027.

A "no" vote is a vote to not levy the tax.

COMPLETE TEXT OF MEASURE R

ORCHARD SCHOOL DISTRICT
Quality Education and Local Control Measure R

INTRODUCTION AND PURPOSE

To provide local school funding that cannot be taken by the State; maintain core academic programs in math, science, reading, technology and the arts; attract and retain highly qualified educators, retain student mental health counselors, social workers, and healthcare professionals, the Orchard School District ("District") proposes to levy a quality education and local control parcel tax for a period of eight years starting on July 1, 2027 at the rate of 4 cents per square foot per parcel per year (not-to-exceed $7,500 non-residential and $89 residential per parcel per year), with an exemption available for senior citizens and certain disabled persons, and to implement accountability measures, to ensure the funds are used to help:

  • Prepare Orchard students for a future shaped by technology, innovation, and rapid change;
  • Create educational opportunities that do not currently exist;
  • Expand access to innovative learning experiences;
  • Enhance the quality of educational programs;
  • Preserve educational opportunities established through this measure;
  • Provide students with future-ready learning experiences that compliment and strengthen the educational opportunities available through existing funding sources;
  • Recruit, hire, train, develop, and retain highly qualified teachers, instructional specialists, and educational experts necessary to establish, deliver, improve, and sustain the educational opportunities described in this measure;
  • Develop future-ready skills through STEAM educational opportunities, including creativity, critical thinking, communication, collaboration, leadership, entrepreneurship, digital citizenship, ethical technology use, problem solving, and career exploration;
  • Maintain and protect strong core academic programs in math, science, reading, technology and visual and performing arts;
  • Strengthen reading and writing skills for all students while providing targeted support based on individual needs;
  • Retain student mental health counselors, social workers, and healthcare professionals;
  • Create, expand, enhance, and preserve exceptional Science, Technology, Engineering, and Mathematics (STEAM) education for every Orchard student, including artificial intelligence literacy, responsible AI use and ethics, computer science and coding, robotics, engineering, hands-on science laboratories, advanced mathematics, visual, design thinking, and creativity and innovation;
  • Provide current instructional materials, curriculum, instructional technology, laboratories, makerspaces, equipment, educational resources, and learning enrolments necessary to establish and sustain the educational opportunities described in this measure;

The proceeds of the parcel tax shall be deposited into a separate account created by the District.

DEFINITION OF "PARCEL"

For purposes of the quality education parcel tax, the term "Parcel" means any parcel of land which lies wholly or partially within the boundaries of the Orchard School District, that receives a separate tax bill for ad valorem property taxes from the Santa Clara County Assessor/Tax Collector, as applicable. All property that is otherwise exempt from or upon which are levied no ad valorem property taxes in any year shall also be exempt from the quality education parcel tax in such year.

For purposes of this quality education and local control parcel tax, any such "Parcels" which are (i) contiguous, and (ii) used solely for owner-occupied, single-family residential purposes, and (iii) held under identical ownership may, by submitting to the District an application of the owners thereof by June 15 of any year, be treated as a single "parcel" for purposes of the levy of the high quality education parcel tax.

EXEMPTION FOR SENIORS AND SSI RECIPIENTS

Pursuant to Government Code Section 50079 (b)(1), any owner of a Parcel used solely for owner-occupied, single-family residential purposes and who are either (a) 65 years of age or older on or before June 30 of the fiscal year immediately preceding the year in which the tax would apply, or (b) persons receiving Supplemental Security Income for a disability, regardless of age, or (c) receiving Social Security Disability Insurance benefits, regardless of age, whose yearly income does not exceed 250 percent of the 2012 federal poverty guidelines issued by the United States Department of Health and Human Services, may obtain an exemption from the parcel tax by submitting an application therefore, by June 15 of any year, to the District. Once granted, exemptions remain in place until the property is transferred or not owner-occupied by a qualifying person.

The District may establish administrative procedures to periodically verify the continuance of any previously granted exemption.

With respect to all general property tax matters within its jurisdiction, the Santa Clara County Treasurer and Tax Collector or other appropriate county tax officials, shall make all final determinations of tax exemption or relief for any reason, and that decision shall be final and binding. With respect to matters specific to the levy of the high quality and local control parcel tax, including any exemptions and the classification of property for purposes of calculating the tax, the decisions of the District shall be final and binding.

REDUCTION IN TAX IF RESULT IS LESS OTHER GOVERNMENT SUPPORT

The collection of the quality education and local control parcel tax is not intended to decrease or offset any increase in local, state or federal government revenue sources that would otherwise be available to the District during the period of the parcel tax. In the event that the levy and collection does have such an effect, the District may cease the levy or shall reduce the parcel tax to the extent that such action would restore the amount of the decrease or offset in other revenues.

ACCOUNTABILITY MEASURES

In accordance with the requirements of Government Code Sections 50075.1 and 50075.3, the following accountability measures, among others, shall apply to the parcel tax levied in accordance with this Measure: (a) the specific purposes of the parcel tax shall be those purposes identified above; (b) the proceeds of the parcel tax shall be applied only to those specific purposes identified above; (c) a separate, special account shall be created into which the proceeds of the quality education and local control parcel tax must be deposited; and (d) an annual written report shall be made to the Board of Trustees of the District showing (i) the amount of funds collected and expended from the proceeds of the quality education and local control parcel tax and (ii) the status of any projects or programs required or authorized to be funded from the proceeds of the parcel tax, as identified above.

ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE R

A Yes vote on Measure R is a community investment in creating, expanding and enhancing educational opportunities to prepare every Orchard School student to succeed in this rapidly changing world.

Measure R will provide funds to:

  • Maintain and protect strong core academic programs in math, science and reading
  • Improve reading and writing skills for all students
  • Attract and retain highly qualified teachers and staff.
  • Enhance and preserve STEAM education
  • Support visual and performing arts
  • Prepare Orchard students for a future shaped by technology and innovation.

Measure R is a 4 cents per building square foot parcel tax, not to exceed $89 for single family homes and apartments. Commercial and industrial properties will also pay 4 cents per square foot with a maximum of $7,500. Senior citizens are eligible for complete exemption.

To ensure transparency, accountability and responsible stewardship of taxpayer funds, the Board of Trustees shall review and approve Measure R expenditures annually during a public meeting. An annual report shall be prepared and made available to the public identifying Measure R revenues and expenditures by category and percentage.

It is the express intent of the Board of Trustees that these locally controlled funds remain separate from the District's general fund, be used exclusively for the purposes set forth in this measure and directly benefit Orchard School District students by preserving and enhancing the high-quality educational programs and opportunities valued by our community.

Quality schools and classrooms protect and enhance our property values and support our students. Measure R's investment in our schools is a great way to protect and preserve our property values.

Please join us and vote Yes on Measure R.
