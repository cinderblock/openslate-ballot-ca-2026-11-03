COUNTY COUNSEL'S IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS OF MEASURE N

California law permits school districts to issue bonds with the approval of 55% of district voters. Such bonds may be used only for construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including furnishing and equipping those facilities, or the acquisition or lease of real property for school facilities.

The Board of Trustees (Board) of the Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District (District) has placed on the ballot a Measure authorizing the issuance and sale of up to $321 million in such bonds.

As identified in the Measure, projects may include but are not limited to:

  • Replacing leaky roofs;
  • Improving school safety with campus security and emergency communications systems;
  • Updating, repairing, and equipping science technology, engineering, arts, math classrooms and labs;
  • Providing up-to-date instructional tools for teachers;
  • Replacing and repairing failing plumbing, electrical, heating, and cooling systems;
  • Improving accessibility for students with disabilities;
  • Updating and upgrading failing school restrooms;
  • Updating athletic facilities for school and community use;
  • Upgrading and modernizing robotics labs and maker spaces for hands-on learning;
  • Making energy-efficiency upgrades;
  • Upgrading or replacing school support facilities including physical education, music, and art classrooms, wood and metal shops, and libraries;
  • Removing hazardous materials like asbestos, mold, and lead paint; and
  • Resurfacing or replacing asphalt, fields, and downspout drainage systems.

Bond proceeds may be spent on eligible project costs, such as acquiring equipment and paying for environmental studies, assessment reviews, and design and construction documentation, as well as engineering, legal, project management, and training costs and expenses. In accordance with state law, the Measure states that bond proceeds would not be used for teacher or school administrator salaries or other operating expenses.

These general obligation bonds will be payable from ad valorem taxes levied on taxable real property within the District. The Measure states that the best estimate of the average annual tax rate to be levied to repay the proposed bonds is $30.00 per $100,000 of assessed value, that the final fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected will be 2056-2057, and that the best estimate of the total debt service during the life of the bond, including principal and interest, is $690 million.

State law requires the District to take certain steps to account for the bond proceeds. Accordingly, the District has listed in the Measure the specific projects to be funded, including a certification that, in developing the list, the Board evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology needs. Additionally, the District will appoint a citizens' oversight committee, conduct annual independent audits, create an account into which proceeds shall be deposited, and comply with state law reporting requirements.

A "yes" vote is a vote to authorize the issuance and sale of up to $321 million of general obligation bonds to fund school facilities projects as described in the Measure.

A "no" vote is a vote not to authorize the issuance and sale of the bonds.

PROJECT LIST

The Board of Trustees of the Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District is committed to maintaining high qualify academics and programs at Los Gatos High School and Saratoga High School by repairing and upgrading aging school facilities and providing safe, secure, modern classrooms, labs and technology needed to support high quality instruction in math, science, engineering, technology, and other skilled trades. To that end, the Board evaluated the District's urgent and critical facility needs, including safety issues, class size reduction, basic repairs, computer and information technology in developing the types of projects to be funded by this measure as described below.

In approving this Project List, the Board of Trustees determines that the District must:

(i) Replace leaky roofs and improve school safety; and

(ii) Provide up-to-date instructional tools for teachers; and

(iii) Replace failing plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling systems; and

(iv) Adhere to specific fiscal accountability safeguards such as:

(a) Every penny must be spent on local high schools and cannot be spent on administrator salaries;

(b) Prohibit the State from taking any of the funds raised;

(c) Require annual independent financial and performance audits; and

(d) Require the review of all expenditures by an independent citizens' oversight committee and public disclosure of all spending.

The Project List includes the following types of upgrades and improvements at District high schools, facilities and sites:

LOCALLY CONTROLLED FUNDING FOR SCHOOL IMPROVEMENTS

School Repair, Safety and Upgrade Projects

Goals and Purposes:

Some high school buildings are over 100 years old. Long-term, it costs more to maintain currently plumbing and energy systems that are well beyond their useful life than to replace them. This measure will replace aging school roofs, windows, plumbing, HVAC and energy systems to avoid costly ongoing repairs. Investing in local school benefits all members of the community whether or not you have school-age children by maintaining high property values.

  • Improve school safety and campus security systems including security fencing, security cameras, and emergency communications systems
  • Upgrade electrical and technology systems and wiring
  • Replace leaky roofs
  • Improve school accessibility for students with disabilities
  • Update and upgrade failing school restrooms
  • Improve student safety at school pick up and drop off areas
  • Update athletic facilities for school and community use.

SUPPORT HIGH QUALITY INSTRUCTION IN MATH, SCIENCE, ENGINEERING AND TECHNOLOGY

Projects That Improve Student Success

Goals and Purposes:

While our students, teachers and academic programs are among the best in the nation, our high school buildings are in desperate need of repairs and updates. This measure will bring classrooms and labs up to the standard necessary to maintain our prestigious academics.

Maintaining our high schools as the best in the State depends on attracting and retaining our high-quality teachers and staff. This measure will make sure our classrooms and technology are up-to-date so that top teacher will want to continuing teaching here.

  • Update, repair and equip science, technology, engineering, arts, math classrooms and labs to support high quality instruction
  • Replace and repair failing plumbing, electrical, heating and cooling systems
  • Keep computer systems and instructional technology up-to-date.
  • Upgrade and modernize robotics labs and maker spaces for hands-on learning

FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY

This Measure requires a clear system of accountability, including a citizen's oversight committee to ensure all funds are spent properly. By law, every penny of this Measure must be spent on our local high schools and no funds can be taken by the state.

In accordance with Education Code Section 15272, the Board of Trustees will appoint a citizens' oversight committee and conduct annual independent audits to ensure that funds are spent only on District projects and for no other purpose. The expenditure of bond money on these projects is subject to stringent financial accountability requirements. By law, performance and financial audits will be performed annually, and all bond expenditures will be monitored by an independent citizens' oversight committee to ensure that funds are spent as promised and specified. The citizens' oversight committee must include, among others, representation of a bona fide taxpayers association, a business organization and a senior citizens organization. No District employees or vendors are allowed to serve on the citizens' oversight committee.

No Administrator Salaries: Proceeds from the sale of the bonds authorized by this proposition shall be used only for the acquisition, construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, and not for any other purpose, including teacher and school administrator salaries and other operating expenses.

TAX RATE STATEMENT CONCERNING MEASURE

An election will be held in the Los Gatos-Saratoga Union High School District (the "District") on November 3, 2026 to authorize the sale of up to $321,000,000 in general obligation bonds. If such bonds are authorized and sold, principal and interest on the bonds will be payable only from the proceeds of ad valorem tax levies made upon the taxable property in the District. The following information is submitted in compliance with Sections 9400-9406 of the Elections Code of the State of California. Such information is based upon the best estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon experience within the District, and other demonstrable factors.

The following information is provided pursuant to Elections Code Section 9401:

1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt service, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of filing of this statement, is $30.00 per $100,000 (3.0¢ per $100) of assessed valuation. It is currently expected that the tax will be collected until fiscal year 2056-57.

2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to be levied to fund this bond issue, for each year that bonds are outstanding, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of filing this statement, is $30.00 per $100,000 (3.0¢ per $100) of assessed valuation, which is projected to be the same in every fiscal year the bonds remain outstanding.

3. The best estimate of total debt service, including principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and sold will be approximately $690 million.

Voters should note that these estimates are based on projections derived from information obtained from official sources, and are based on the assessed value (not market value) of taxable property on the County's official tax rolls. Property owners should consult their own property tax bills and tax advisors to determine their property's assessed value and any applicable tax exemptions.

The attention of all voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information is based upon projections and estimates only, which amounts are not maximum amounts and durations and are not binding upon the District. The actual debt service, tax rates and the years in which they will apply may vary depending on the timing of bond sales, the par amount of bonds sold at each sale and actual increases in assessed valuations. The timing of the bond sales and the amount of bonds sold at any given time will be determined by the District based on the need for project funds and other considerations. Actual assessed valuations will depend upon the amount and value of taxable property within the District as determined by the County Assessor in the annual assessment and the equalization process.
