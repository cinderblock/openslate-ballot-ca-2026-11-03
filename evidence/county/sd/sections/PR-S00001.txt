p81 COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p81 The Governing Board for the Fallbrook Union Elementary School District
p81 (“District”) placed this measure on the ballot. If approved by 55% of the votes
p81 cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general
p81 obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $76,000,000.
p81 This measure will authorize a levy on the assessed value of taxable
p81 property within the geographical boundaries of the District in an amount
p81 needed to pay the principal and interest on the bonds each year the bonds
p81 are outstanding. The District estimates the average tax rate would be $30
p81 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be
p81 approximately $30 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is
p81 anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2062-2063.
p81 The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required
p81 to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $167,000,000.
p81 Actual amounts may vary depending on the timing of sales, bonds sold at
p81 each sale, interest rates, and assessed valuation.
p81 The measure identifies a list of projects at schools and facilities within the
p81 District that may be funded from the bond proceeds. Approval of this
p81 measure does not guarantee the proposed projects will be funded by the
p81 sale of the bonds. The District’s proposal for certain projects may assume
p81 receipt of matching State funds subject to appropriation by the Legislature
p81 or approval of a statewide bond measure.
p81 As required by State law, the measure states that the proceeds from the
p81 sale of the bonds may be used only for the purposes specified and that the
p81 District will establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee and
p81 conduct an annual independent performance audit to ensure funds are spent
p81 only on the projects listed in the measure. The District must also conduct an
p81 annual, independent financial audit of bond proceeds. The District states it
p81 has evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology
p81 needs in developing the projects listed in the measure.
p81 A “YES” vote is in favor of authorizing the District to issue and sell
p81 $76,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p81 A “NO” vote is against authorizing the District to issue and sell
p81 $76,000,000 in general obligation bonds.

p81 An election will be held in Fallbrook Union Elementary School District
p81 (the “District”) on November 3, 2026 for the purpose of submitting to the
p81 electors of the District the question of incurring bonded indebtedness of the
p81 District in the principal amount of $76,000,000. If such bonds are authorized
p81 and sold, the principal thereof and interest thereon will be payable from the
p81 proceeds of taxes levied on the taxable property in the District. The following
p81 information regarding tax rates is given in compliance with Section 9401 of
p81 the California Elections Code. This information is based upon the best
p81 estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon
p81 experience within the District and other demonstrable factors.
p81 Based upon the foregoing and projections of the assessed valuations
p81 of taxable property in the District, and assuming the entire debt service on
p81 the bonds will be paid through property taxation:
p81 1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be
p81 required to be levied to fund the bond issue over the entire duration of the
p81 bond debt service, based on assessed valuations available at the time of the
p81 filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience within the same
p81 jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors is $30.00 per $100,000 of assessed
p81 valuation of all property to be taxed. It is estimated that the final fiscal year in
p81 which the tax is anticipated to be collected is fiscal year 2062-63.
p81 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to
p81 be levied to fund the bond issue, and an estimate of the year in which that
p81 rate will apply, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time
p81 of the filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience within the
p81 same jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors, is $30.00 per $100,000 of
p81 assessed valuation, which is projected to be the same in every fiscal year
p81 the bonds remain outstanding.
p81 3. The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal
p81 and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued
p81 and sold is approximately $167,000,000.
p81 Attention of voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information
p81 is based upon projections and estimates only. The actual timing of sales of
p81 the bonds and the amount to be sold at any time will be governed by the
p81 needs of the District and other factors. The actual interest rates at which the
p81 bonds will be sold, which will not exceed the maximum permitted by law, will
p81 depend upon the bond market at the time of sale. The actual assessed
p81 valuations in future years will depend upon the value of property within the
p81 District as determined in the assessment and the equalization process.
p81 Assessed valuation is not the same as market price of real property.
p81 Therefore, the actual tax rates and the years in which those tax rates will be
p81 applicable may vary from those presently estimated and stated above.
p81 Voters should note that estimated tax rates are based on the
p81 ASSESSED VALUE of taxable property on the County’s official tax rolls, not
p81 on the property’s market value. Property owners should consult their own
p81 property tax bills to determine their property’s assessed value and any
p81 applicable tax exemptions.

p84 FISCAL ACCOUNTABILITY
p84 THIS MEASURE REQUIRES A CLEAR SYSTEM OF
p84 ACCOUNTABILITY, INCLUDING A PROJECT LIST DETAILING HOW
p84 THE MONEY WILL BE USED, A CITIZENS’ OVERSIGHT COMMITTEE,
p84 AND INDEPENDENT AUDITS TO ENSURE MONEY IS SPENT
p84 PROPERLY.
p84 In accordance with Education Code Section 15272, the Governing
p84 Board will appoint a citizens’ oversight committee and conduct annual
p84 independent audits to ensure that funds are spent only on District projects
p84 and for no other purpose. The expenditure of bond money on these projects
p84 is subject to stringent financial accountability requirements. By law,
p84 performance and financial audits will be performed annually, and all bond
p84 expenditures will be monitored by an independent citizens’ oversight
p84 committee to ensure that funds are spent as promised and specified. The
p84 citizens’ oversight committee must include, among others, representation of
p84 a bona fide taxpayers’ association, a business organization and a senior
p84 citizens organization. No District employees or vendors are allowed to serve
p84 on the citizens’ oversight committee.
p84 No Administrator Salaries: Proceeds from the sale of the bonds
p84 authorized by this proposition shall be used only for the acquisition,
p84 construction, reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school
p84 facilities, including the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, and not
p84 for any other purpose, including teacher and school administrator salaries
p84 and other operating expenses.
p84 State Matching Funds. The following statement is included in this
p84 measure pursuant to Education Code Section 15122.5: Approval of Measure

p84 K. The school district’s proposal for the project or projects may assume the
p84 receipt of matching state funds, which could be subject to appropriation by
