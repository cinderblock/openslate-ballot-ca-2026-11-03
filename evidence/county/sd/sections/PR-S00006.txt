p79 COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p79 The Board of Education for the Mountain Empire Unified School District
p79 (“District”) placed this measure on the ballot. If approved by 55% of the votes
p79 cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general
p79 obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $23,000,000.
p79 This measure will authorize a levy on the assessed value of taxable
p79 property within the geographical boundaries of the District in an amount
p79 needed to pay the principal and interest on the bonds each year the bonds
p79 are outstanding. The District estimates the average tax rate would be $38
p79 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be
p79 approximately $38 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is
p79 anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2060-2061.
p79 The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required
p79 to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $47,978,702.
p79 Actual amounts may vary depending on the timing of sales, bonds sold at
p79 each sale, interest rates, and assessed valuation.
p79 The measure identifies a list of projects at schools and facilities within the
p79 District that may be funded from the bond proceeds. Approval of this
p79 measure does not guarantee the proposed projects will be funded by the
p79 sale of the bonds. The District’s proposal for certain projects may assume
p79 receipt of matching State funds subject to appropriation by the Legislature
p79 or approval of a statewide bond measure.
p79 As required by State law, the measure states that the proceeds from the
p79 sale of the bonds may be used only for the purposes specified and that the
p79 District will establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee and
p79 conduct an annual independent performance audit to ensure funds are spent
p79 only on the projects listed in the measure. The District must also conduct an
p79 annual, independent financial audit of bond proceeds. The District states it
p79 has evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology
p79 needs in developing the projects listed in the measure.
p79 A “YES” vote is in favor of authorizing the District to issue and sell
p79 $23,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p79 A “NO” vote is against authorizing the District to issue and sell
p79 $23,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p79 TAX INFORMATION STATEMENT
p79 An election will be held in the Mountain Empire Unified School District
p79 (the “District”) on November 3, 2026, to authorize the sale of up to
p79 $23,000,000 in bonds of the District to finance school facilities as described
p79 in the measure. If the bond measure is approved by at least 55% of the
p79 voters of the District voting on the bond measure, the District expects to
p79 issue the bonds in multiple series over time. Principal and interest on the
p79 bonds will be payable from the proceeds of tax levies made upon the taxable
p79 property in the District. The following information is provided in compliance
p79 with Sections 9400 through 9405 of the California Elections Code.
p79 1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would
p79 be required to be levied to fund this bond issue over the entire
p79 duration of the bond debt service, based on assessed valuations
p79 available at the time of filing this statement, is $38.00 per $100,000
p79 of assessed valuation. The final fiscal year in which the tax to be
p79 levied to fund this bond issue is anticipated to be collected is fiscal
p79 year 2060-61.
p79 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be
p79 required to be levied to fund this bond issue, based on estimated
p79 assessed valuations available at the time of filing this statement, is
p79 $38.00 per $100,000 of assessed valuation in fiscal year 2027-28.
p79 3. The best estimate of the total debt service, including the
p79 principal and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all of the
p79 bonds are issued and sold is approximately $47,978,702.
p79 Voters should note that estimated tax rates are based on the
p79 ASSESSED VALUE of taxable property on the County’s official tax rolls, not

p80 ACCOUNTABILITY SAFEGUARDS
p80 The provisions in this section are specifically included in this measure
p80 in order that the voters and taxpayers of the District may be assured that
p80 their money will be spent to address specific school facilities needs of the
p80 District, all in compliance with the requirements of Article XIIIA, Section
p80 1(b)(3) of the Constitution of the State of California (the “California
p80 Constitution”), and the Strict Accountability in Local School Construction
p80 Bonds Act of 2000 (codified at Sections 15264 and following of the California

p80 Evaluation of Needs. The Board of Education of the District (the
p80 “Board”) has evaluated the facilities needs of the District to determine which
p80 projects to finance from a local bond at this time. To address the facilities
p80 needs of the District, the Board deems it necessary and advisable to fund
p80 the specific school facilities projects listed in the Bond Project List. The
p80 Board hereby certifies that it has evaluated safety, class size reduction and
p80 information technology needs in developing the Bond Project List.
p80 Limitations on Use of Bonds. Proceeds from the sale of bonds
p80 authorized by this measure shall be used only for the construction,
p80 reconstruction, rehabilitation, or replacement of school facilities, including
p80 the furnishing and equipping of school facilities, or the acquisition or lease
p80 of real property for school facilities, and not for any other purpose, including
p80 teacher and administrator salaries and other school operating expenses.
p80 More specifically, the Bond Project List provides for the specific projects the
p80 District proposes to finance with proceeds from the sale of bonds authorized
p80 by this measure and such proceeds shall be applied only to those specific
p80 purposes.

p80 shall establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee, within 60 days
p80 of the date that the Board enters the election results on its minutes pursuant
p80 to Section 15274 of the California Education Code, to ensure that (a) bond
p80 revenues are expended only for the purposes specified in Article XIIIA,
p80 Section 1(b)(3) of the California Constitution, and (b) that no funds are used
p80 for any teacher or administrative salaries or other school operating
p80 expenses. In accordance with Section 15282 of the California Education
p80 Code, the citizens’ oversight committee shall consist of at least seven
p80 members and shall include a member active in a business organization
p80 representing the business community located within the District, a member
p80 active in a senior citizens’ organization, a member active in a bona fide
p80 taxpayers’ organization, a member that is a parent or guardian of a child
p80 enrolled in the District, and a member that is both a parent or guardian of a
p80 child enrolled in the District and active in a parent-teacher organization. The
p80 committee may be comprised of the same members of the citizens’ oversight
p80 committees for other District bond measures. No employee or official of the
p80 District and no vendor, contractor or consultant of the District shall be
p80 appointed to the citizens’ oversight committee.
