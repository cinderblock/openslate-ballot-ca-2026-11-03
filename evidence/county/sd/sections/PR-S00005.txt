p83 COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p83 The Board of Education for the La Mesa-Spring Valley School District
p83 (“District”) placed this measure on the ballot. If approved by 55% of the votes
p83 cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general
p83 obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $131,000,000.
p83 This measure will authorize a levy on the assessed value of taxable
p83 property within the geographical boundaries of the District in an amount
p83 needed to pay the principal and interest on the bonds each year the bonds
p83 are outstanding. The District estimates the average tax rate would be $21.70
p83 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be
p83 approximately $21.70 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is
p83 anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2056-2057.
p83 The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required
p83 to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $257,998,000.
p83 Actual amounts may vary depending on the timing of sales, bonds sold at
p83 each sale, interest rates, and assessed valuation.
p83 The measure identifies a list of projects at schools and facilities within the
p83 District that may be funded from the bond proceeds. Approval of this
p83 measure does not guarantee the proposed projects will be funded by the
p83 sale of the bonds. The District’s proposal for certain projects may assume
p83 receipt of matching State funds subject to appropriation by the Legislature
p83 or approval of a statewide bond measure.
p83 As required by State law, the measure states that the proceeds from the
p83 sale of the bonds may be used only for the purposes specified and that the
p83 District will establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee and
p83 conduct an annual independent performance audit to ensure funds are spent
p83 only on the projects listed in the measure. The District must also conduct an
p83 annual, independent financial audit of bond proceeds. The District states it
p83 has evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology
p83 needs in developing the projects listed in the measure.
p83 A “YES” vote is in favor of authorizing the District to issue and sell
p83 $131,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p83 A “NO” vote is against authorizing the District to issue and sell
p83 $131,000,000 in general obligation bonds.

p83 in the principal amount of $131,000,000. If such bonds are authorized and

p83 proceeds of taxes levied on the taxable property in the District. The following
p83 information regarding tax rates is given in compliance with Section 9401 of
p83 the California Elections Code. This information is based upon the best
p83 estimates and projections presently available from official sources, upon
p83 experience within the District and other demonstrable factors.
p83 Based upon the foregoing and projections of the assessed valuations
p83 of taxable property in the District, and assuming the entire debt service on
p83 the bonds will be paid through property taxation:
p83 1. The best estimate of the average annual tax rate that would be
p83 required to be levied to fund the bond issue over the entire duration of the
p83 bond debt service, based on assessed valuations available at the time of the
p83 filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience within the same
p83 jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors, is $21.70 per $100,000 of
p83 assessed valuation of all property to be taxed. It is estimated that the final
p83 fiscal year in which the tax is anticipated to be collected is fiscal year
p83 2056-57.
p83 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to
p83 be levied to fund the bond issue, and an estimate of the year in which that
p83 rate will apply, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time
p83 of the filing of this statement, or a projection based on experience within the
p83 same jurisdiction or other demonstrable factors, is $21.70 per $100,000 of
p83 assessed valuation of all property to be taxed for fiscal years 2028-29
p83 through and including 2056-57.
p83 3. The best estimate of the total debt service, including the principal
p83 and interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued
p83 and sold is approximately $257,998,000.
p83 Attention of voters is directed to the fact that the foregoing information
p83 is based upon projections and estimates only. The actual timing of sales of
p83 the bonds and the amount to be sold at any time will be governed by the
p83 needs of the District and other factors. The actual interest rates at which the
p83 bonds will be sold, which will not exceed the maximum permitted by law, will
p83 depend upon the bond market at the time of sale. The actual assessed
p83 valuations in future years will depend upon the value of property within the
p83 District as determined in the assessment and the equalization process.
p83 Assessed valuation is not the same as market price of real property.
p83 Therefore, the actual tax rates and the years in which those tax rates will be
p83 applicable may vary from those presently estimated and stated above.
p83 Voters should note that estimated tax rates are based on the
p83 ASSESSED VALUE of taxable property on the County’s official tax rolls, not
p83 on the property’s market value. Property owners should consult their own
p83 property tax bills to determine their property’s assessed value and any
p83 applicable tax exemptions.

p84 The following is the full proposition presented to the voters by the La
p84 Mesa-Spring Valley School District (the “District”). In connection with this
p84 bond proposition, the Board of Education (the “Board”) of the District has
p84 made the following findings:
p84 FINDINGS
p84 •
p84 The District provides quality education for more than 10,000 local
p84 students in preschool through eighth grade that prepares them for
p84 success in high school, college and future careers.
p84 •
p84 With most local schools built more than 60 years ago, the District
p84 is working to ensure all students learn in classrooms that meet
p84 current educational standards as well as fire, earthquake, security
p84 and safety standards.
p84 •
p84 Some schools were built so long ago that underground clay and
p84 cast-iron pipes and infrastructure are failing, resulting in gas leaks,
p84 water leaks and sewage backups.
p84 •
p84 During warmer months, classroom temperatures can exceed 90
p84 degrees, requiring air conditioning and outdoor shade structures
p84 to keep students cool and safe.
p84 •
p84 A thorough assessment of each classroom and school facility has
p84 identified high-priority repairs and upgrades.
p84 •
p84 The District has developed a thorough and comprehensive
p84 Facilities Master Plan to complete the highest priority repairs and
p84 upgrades needed at each school campus.
p84 •
p84 Because the State does not provide dedicated funding to school
p84 districts for repairing and upgrading school facilities and bond
p84 measures are the primary means by which local school districts
p84 fund these needs, the Board believes that locally controlled
p84 funding from a school improvement bond measure is needed to
p84 repair and upgrade aging local schools.
p84 •
p84 With an old bond measure set to expire, the District has an
p84 opportunity to issue new bonds by extending, but not increasing,
p84 the tax rate paid by local property owners.
p84 •
p84 The proposed bond extension measure is not projected to
p84 increase tax rates beyond the amount local property owners
p84 currently pay.
p84 •
p84 If approved by local voters, the extension measure would
p84 generate $131 million in locally controlled funding by extending
p84 the current bond tax rate previously approved by voters.
p84 •
p84 Funding from the extension measure would be used to repair or
p84 replace deteriorating roofs, plumbing, sewer and electrical
p84 systems.
p84 •
p84 Funds from the extension measure would also install shade
p84 structures and air conditioning to protect students from extreme
p84 heat.
p84 •
p84 Funds from the extension measure would improve student safety
p84 and campus security, including security locks, emergency
p84 communications systems, security cameras, smoke detectors, fire
p84 alarms and sprinklers.
p84 •
p84 Funds from the extension measure would upgrade classrooms,
p84 labs, technology and multipurpose rooms to support programs in
p84 math, science, reading, writing, arts and music.
p84 •
p84 Funds from the extension measure would remove hazardous
p84 materials like asbestos and lead pipes from older schools, and
p84 upgrade older schools to meet current health codes, building
p84 safety codes and improve access for students with disabilities.
p84 •
p84 Voter approval of the bond extension measure will help local
p84 schools qualify for an estimated $61 million in state matching
p84 funds that will otherwise will go to improve schools in other
p84 communities.
