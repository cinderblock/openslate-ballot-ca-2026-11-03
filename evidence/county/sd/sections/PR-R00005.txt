p79 CITY ATTORNEY IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p79 1% GENERAL TRANSACTIONS AND USE (SALES) TAX FOR
p79 TEN (10) YEARS
p79 On June 24, 2026, the City Council voted to place Measure “Y” on the
p79 November 3, 2026 General Municipal Election ballot.
p79 If approved by a majority of Santee voters, this Measure would authorize a
p79 one percent (1%) retail transactions and use (sales) tax within the City of
p79 Santee. It is estimated that Measure “Y” will provide an additional
p79 $14,000,000 in annual local funding for ten (10) years that could be used for
p79 City of Santee services and infrastructure, including repairing streets and
p79 potholes; cleaning and maintaining streets, sidewalks, parks and public
p79 facilities, repairing and replacing stormdrains, local traffic management, fire
p79 protection, paramedic, crime prevention, and 911 emergency response. This
p79 tax would be a “general tax,” meaning that revenues raised from the tax
p79 would go into the City’s general fund to pay for any lawful City program,
p79 improvement, or service.
p79 The California Constitution (Proposition 218) and Revenue and Taxation
p79 Code section 7285.9 authorize the City to levy a general transactions and
p79 use/sales tax at a rate of one percent (1%) so long as the tax is approved by
p79 a majority of the voters voting in an election on that issue. If approved, the
p79 tax would become operative on April 1, 2027 and remain in effect for ten (10)
p79 years until it expires on March 31, 2037. Any extension of this tax beyond
p79 that date would require majority voter approval at a future City election.
p79 Measure “Y” establishes a five (5) member Citizens’ Oversight Committee
p79 consisting of Santee residents, property owners and business owners
p79 appointed by the City Council. The Committee shall hold meetings open to
p79 the public pursuant to the California Brown Act. Each year, City staff shall
p79 present a proposed annual spending plan to the Committee, identifying City
p79 improvements, projects, programs, services proposed to be funded with
p79 Measure “Y” revenues. The Committee may make recommendations about
p79 the spending plan, which shall be incorporated into the City Manager’s
p79 proposed annual budget and subject to City Council approval.
p79 The Committee shall also be authorized to independently review the City’s
p79 annual financial audit identifying Measure “Y” revenues collected and the
p79 expenditures made from such revenues in the prior year. The Committee
p79 shall report their findings publicly, including any concerns over use of
p79 Measure “Y” revenues, through an annual report presented to the City
p79 Council at a public meeting.
p79 The tax would be paid in addition to current sales taxes and would be
p79 collected at the same time and in the same manner as existing sales taxes.
p79 All revenues raised by the tax would remain in the City and would not be
p79 shared with the State, County or any other agency.
p79 A “yes” vote on Measure “Y” will authorize the 1% transactions and use
p79 (sales) tax for 10 years.
p79 A “no” vote on Measure “Y” will not authorize the 1% transactions and use
p79 (sales) tax for 10 years.
p79 The above statement is an impartial analysis of Measure “Y”. If you
p79 desire a copy of the Measure, please call the City Clerk’s Office at
p79 (619) 258-4100 or email at clerk@cityofsanteeca.gov and a copy will be
p79 sent at no cost to you.

p80 ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE Y
p80 Some will say the City should live within its means. It does. Santee runs a
p80 lean, conservative budget, and the San Diego County Taxpayers
p80 Association agrees as noted in their endorsement of the measure.
p80 Santee’s infrastructure needs have grown while the operating budget has
p80 not kept pace. We all drive the same streets. We all see the potholes and
p80 the wear on our roads. And we all expect that when we call 911, help arrives
p80 fast. These are services residents count on every day.
p80 Measure Y is a one cent local sales tax. By state law it cannot be charged
p80 on groceries or prescription medications, so it does not fall on necessities.
p80 Visitors who shop here help pay too. A resident advisory committee and
p80 independent polling set the priorities for funding:
p80 •
p80 Citywide pavement repairs
p80 •
p80 New Fire Station at Woodglen Vista to address response times
p80 •
p80 Renovation of 55 year old Fire Station 4 (Cottonwood Ave)
p80 •
p80 Two new fire engines
p80 •
p80 Citywide storm drain replacements
p80 •
p80 Restoration of 8 city bridges
p80 •
p80 Citywide park improvements
p80 The measure establishes an independent citizen oversight committee to
p80 safeguard spending and the tax would sunset after 10 years.
p80 The cost of doing nothing is NOT zero. Every year that major projects are
p80 delayed, construction costs increase, infrastructure continues to deteriorate,
p80 and future solutions become more expensive.
p80 Measure Y is how Santee gets ahead of this: repair what is failing now,
p80 before it becomes a costly emergency.
p80 Vote YES on Measure Y.

p80 REBUTTAL TO THE ARGUMENT IN FAVOR OF MEASURE Y
p80 Don’t be fooled, Santee friends and neighbors!
p80 The City wants to minimize Measure Y by calling it a “1-cent” tax. While
p80 technically true, it means 1% of every taxable dollar you spend for the next
p80 10 years! Overnight, gas will cost 5 cents more per gallon, a new car another
p80 $500, and everyday shopping and dining will increase. For Santee families,
p80 those added costs will total thousands over the life of this tax!
p80 But the bigger question is: Can we trust the City to responsibly manage the
p80 money it already receives? An independent July 2026 audit of City travel
p80 expenses found significant policy violations, including the mayor,
p80 councilmembers, and staff receiving mileage payments while also receiving
p80 taxpayer-funded monthly car allowances.
p80 If the City struggles to follow basic financial policies, how can we trust it with
p80 millions more of our hard-earned money?
p80 The City also promotes an “independent” oversight committee as a
p80 safeguard. But its members are appointed by the very Council proposing this
p80 tax. The committee has no authority to stop or reject how the Council spends
p80 the money. It can only make recommendations, while the Council retains
p80 final say - including spending on things unrelated to the projects they claim
p80 this tax would fund. That’s hardly independent or accountable.
p80 Santee families deserve competency and accountability at City Hall. Shifting
p80 the burden to the residents is not the answer!
p80 Vote NO on Measure Y to keep Santee affordable and ensure city leaders
p80 remain accountable!
