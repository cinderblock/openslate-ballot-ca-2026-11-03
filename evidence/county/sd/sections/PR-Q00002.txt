p71 COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p71 MEASURE TO FUND SAN DIEGO COUNTY HEALTH & SAFETY
p71 SERVICES
p71 This measure was placed on the ballot by a petition signed by the requisite
p71 number of voters.
p71 This measure, if approved by a majority vote of qualified electors, would
p71 impose a transaction and use tax of a half cent per dollar on the sale of all
p71 tangible personal property in the County of San Diego, in both incorporated
p71 and unincorporated areas. The tax would be administered similarly to
p71 collection of other state and local sales taxes.
p71 The tax is subject to certain specified exemptions and exclusions that are
p71 generally consistent with state and locally imposed sales taxes. The sales
p71 tax would remain in effect until repealed by voters. It is estimated that the
p71 sales tax would generate approximately $400,000,000 to $450,000,000 per
p71 year.
p71 This measure would establish a Protect San Diego County’s Health and
p71 Safety Fund for revenues generated. Funds would only be used for specified
p71 purposes, including efforts to stop sewage pollution in the Tijuana River
p71 Valley, wildfire prevention and emergency response, access to healthcare,
p71 food assistance, and childhood development services, and authorized
p71 administrative expenses. The measure would allocate funding as follows:
p71 • Stopping sewage pollution in the Tijuana River Valley: 22.5%
p71 • Public safety services and wildfire prevention: 17.5%
p71 • Healthcare, childcare, and nutrition services: 60%
p71 The measure would establish a twelve-member oversight committee, which
p71 would audit how funds are spent to ensure compliance with the measure’s
p71 requirements. The Board of Supervisors for the County of San Diego would
p71 appoint eleven of the twelve members of this committee, who would be
p71 required to have certain qualifications specified in the measure. The twelfth
p71 member of this committee would be the County of San Diego Auditor. The
p71 measure would also prohibit the use of funds for such things as elected
p71 official or County employee salaries, lobbyist contracts, or government office
p71 renovations. The measure would require public disclosure of all spending.
p71 A YES vote would impose a half-cent sales tax for use to clean-up
p71 sewage pollution in the Tijuana River Valley, public safety, wildfire
p71 prevention, healthcare, childcare and nutrition services.
p71 A NO vote would reject a half-cent sales tax for use to clean-up sewage
p71 pollution in the Tijuana River Valley, public safety, wildfire prevention,
p71 healthcare, childcare and nutrition services.

p73 The people of the County of San Diego ordain as follows:
p73 Section 1. Title
p73 This measure shall be known and may be cited as the “Protect San
p73 Diego County’s Health and Safety Act.”
p73 Section 2. Findings
p73 The people of the County of San Diego hereby find and declare the
p73 following:
p73 (a) San Diego County residents’ health, safety, and quality of life
p73 are facing severe threats that Washington D.C. and Sacramento are either
p73 ignoring or making worse.
p73 (b) The Tijuana River toxic sewage crisis is one of America’s worst
p73 environmental disasters that’s sickening entire communities and polluting
p73 our treasured coastline – and state and federal politicians and bureaucrats
p73 have failed to act time and again.
p73 (c) Extreme weather is causing more severe and frequent wildfires
p73 and flooding while the federal government has threatened to withhold fire
p73 aid and disaster relief from California communities.
p73 (d) Severe cuts enacted by Congress to Medicaid, the Affordable
p73 Care Act, and the Supplemental Nutrition Assistance Program will lead to
p73 over 150,000 San Diegans losing healthcare and food they need to survive
p73 and stay healthy.
p73 (e) Those same federal cuts will dramatically reduce the County of
p73 San Diego’s overall budget which puts local 911 first responder staffing
p73 levels and essential public safety services in our neighborhoods at risk.
p73 (f) Children in San Diego County are under double threat from
p73 federal cuts to nutrition and healthcare and the lack of affordable,
p73 accessible childcare vital to children’s healthy development.
p73 (g) This measure will protect San Diego County from the severe
p73 threats we’re facing with urgent, local action that safeguards our
p73 community’s health, safety, and well-being.
p73 (h) All funds from this measure are guaranteed to address San
p73 Diego County’s most urgent health and safety priorities: the Tijuana River
p73 Sewage Crisis, Public Safety, Healthcare and Children’s Healthy
p73 Development – nothing else.
p73 (i) This measure requires public disclosure of spending to track
p73 every dollar raised with the strictest accountability and transparency
p73 requirements, including annual independent fiscal and performance audits
p73 and a citizen oversight committee.
p73 (j) Tough taxpayer protections prohibit funds from this measure to
p73 be used for politicians’ salaries, lobbyist contracts, or government office
p73 renovations. If politicians or bureaucrats try to misuse these funds they
p73 may be subject to criminal prosecution.
p73 (k) The measure’s funding will stay 100% local to our region with an
p73 ironclad legal prohibition on Washington or Sacramento taking even one
p73 dime away from San Diego County.

p73 and improve the health and safety of County residents by doing the
p73 following:
p73 (a) Stopping the Tijuana River toxic sewage flows contaminating
p73 local beaches, bays, and coastal waters with cross-border sewage
p73 treatment infrastructure that we in San Diego County control. No more
p73 waiting for Washington D.C. or Mexico.
