p74 COUNTY COUNSEL IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p74 The Board of Education for the Julian Union High School District
p74 (“District”) placed this measure on the ballot. If approved by 55% of the votes
p74 cast, this measure will authorize the District to issue and sell general
p74 obligation bonds in an amount not to exceed $9,000,000.
p74 This measure will authorize a levy on the assessed value of taxable
p74 property within the geographical boundaries of the District in an amount
p74 needed to pay the principal and interest on the bonds each year the bonds
p74 are outstanding. The District estimates the average tax rate would be $30
p74 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation, and the highest tax rate would be
p74 approximately $30 for every $100,000 of assessed valuation. The tax is
p74 anticipated to be collected through fiscal year 2054-2055.
p74 The estimated total debt service, including principal and interest, required
p74 to be repaid if the bonds are issued and sold is approximately $18,215,253.
p74 Actual amounts may vary depending on the timing of sales, bonds sold at
p74 each sale, interest rates, and assessed valuation.
p74 The measure identifies a list of projects at schools and facilities within the
p74 District that may be funded from the bond proceeds. Approval of this
p74 measure does not guarantee the proposed projects will be funded by the
p74 sale of the bonds. The District’s proposal for certain projects may assume
p74 receipt of matching State funds subject to appropriation by the Legislature
p74 or approval of a statewide bond measure.
p74 As required by State law, the measure states that the proceeds from the
p74 sale of the bonds may be used only for the purposes specified and that the
p74 District will establish an independent citizens’ oversight committee and
p74 conduct an annual independent performance audit to ensure funds are spent
p74 only on the projects listed in the measure. The District must also conduct an
p74 annual, independent financial audit of bond proceeds. The District states it
p74 has evaluated safety, class size reduction, and information technology
p74 needs in developing the projects listed in the measure.
p74 A “YES” vote is in favor of authorizing the District to issue and sell
p74 $9,000,000 in general obligation bonds.
p74 A “NO” vote is against authorizing the District to issue and sell $9,000,000
p74 in general obligation bonds.

p74 “District”) on November 3, 2026, to authorize the sale of up to $9,000,000 in
p74 general obligation bonds. The following information is submitted in
p74 compliance with Sections 9400-9404 of the Elections Code of the State of
p74 California. Such information is based upon the best estimates and
p74 projections presently available from official sources, upon experience within
p74 the District, and other demonstrable factors.

p74 valuation, the following information is provided:

p74 required to fund this bond issue over the entire duration of the bond debt
p74 service, based on estimated assessed valuations available at the time of
p74 filing of this statement, is $30 per $100,000 of assessed valuation. It is
p74 currently expected that the tax will be collected until fiscal year 2054-2055.
p74 2. The best estimate of the highest tax rate that would be required to
p74 fund this bond issue, based on estimated assessed valuations available at
p74 the time of filing this statement, is $30 per $100,000 of assessed valuation.

p74 interest, that would be required to be repaid if all the bonds are issued and
p74 sold will be approximately $18,215,253.

p75 KEY FINDINGS
p75 Julian Union High School District (the “District”) has served our
p75 community for over 100 years, providing generations of students with a well-
p75 balanced education. The Board of Education of the District (the “Board”)
p75 believes that it is important that our students have the same educational
p75 opportunities as others in the region.
p75 To maintain this standard, the District is identifying ways to support
p75 21st-century learning in career technical education opportunities, including
p75 in agricultural science, hands-on mechanics welding, auto shop and animal
p75 programs.
p75 As our buildings continue to age, there is a growing need to ensure our
p75 career technology facilities are safe, functional and aligned with current
p75 industry standards, and to support and improve programs that provide
p75 career technical education opportunities.
p75 Updating and modernizing career technical educational facilities that
p75 focus on hands-on agriculture, auto shop and animal sciences will help
p75 provide District students with the specialized training and competitive edge
p75 needed for success in modern technical careers, and continue to serve our
p75 community for generations to come.
p75 BOND AUTHORIZATION
p75 By approval of this measure by at least 55% of the registered voters
p75 voting on the measure, the District will be authorized to issue and sell bonds
p75 of up to $9,000,000 in aggregate principal amount, at interest rates not to
p75 exceed legal limits and to provide financing for the specific types of school
p75 facilities projects listed in the Bond Project List included below, subject to all
p75 the accountability requirements specified below.
p75 ACCOUNTABILITY REQUIREMENTS
p75 The provisions in this section are specifically included in this measure
p75 in order that the voters and taxpayers in the District may be assured that
p75 their money will be spent wisely. Expenditures to address specific facilities
p75 needs of the District will be in compliance with the requirements of
p75 Article XIIIA, Section 1(b)(3), of the State Constitution and the Strict
p75 Accountability in Local School Construction Bonds Act of 2000 (codified at
p75 Education Code Sections 15264 and following.)
p75 Bonds authorized by this measure may be issued under the provisions
p75 of the California Education Code (starting at Section 15100), under the
p75 provisions of the California Government Code (starting at Section 53506), or
p75 under any other provision of law authorizing the issuance of general
p75 obligation bonds by school districts. The bonds may be issued in series by
p75 the District, from time to time, and each series of bonds shall mature within
p75 the legal limitations set forth in the applicable law under which the bonds are
p75 issued.
p75 Evaluation of Needs. The Board has identified specific facilities needs
p75 of the District, and has determined which projects to finance from a local
p75 bond. The Board hereby certifies that it has evaluated safety, class size
p75 reduction, enrollment growth, and information technology needs in
p75 developing the Bond Project List described below.
p75 Independent Citizens’ Oversight Committee. Following approval of
p75 this measure, the Board will establish an independent citizens’ oversight
p75 committee, under Education Code Sections 15278 and following, to ensure
p75 bond proceeds are expended only on the types of school facilities projects
p75 listed below. The committee will be established within 60 days of the date
p75 when the results of the election appear in the minutes of the Board.
p75 Performance Audits. The Board will conduct annual, independent
p75 performance audits to ensure that the bond proceeds have been expended
p75 only on the school facilities projects listed below.
