p76 MEASURE D
p76 Shall the measure, increasing the permissible height of “multiple-family
p76 developments” in the City of Coronado’s R-4 Multiple Family Residential Zone
p76 from the existing maximum of 3 stories (with a maximum height of no more than
p76 35 feet) to a maximum of 4 stories (with a maximum height of no more than 42
p76 feet), be adopted?
p76 This measure requires Simple Majority (50%+1) of those voting on the measure.
p76 IMPARTIAL ANALYSIS
p76 The City Council of the City of Coronado placed Measure D on the ballot for
p76 voter consideration at the November 3, 2026, election.
p76 Coronado Municipal Code (“CMC”) Section 86.56.048 and Section B-2 of
p76 Part B of Chapter V of the Orange Avenue Corridor Specific Plan (“OACSP”)
p76 currently limit the height of multiple-family developments in the R-4 Multiple-
p76 Family Residential Zone (“R-4 Zone”) to a maximum building height of three
p76 stories with a maximum 35-foot height limit (“Existing Height Limit”).
p76 As part of the City’s Sixth Cycle Housing Element Update adoption
p76 (“Housing Element Update”), the California Department of Housing and
p76 Community Development (“HCD”) took the position that the Existing Height
p76 Limit constitutes a constraint on developments seeking to obtain the
p76 permitted density of 40 dwelling units per acre. Further, per HCD’s guidance,
p76 a zoning district must allow at least one story and 10 feet of height for every
p76 10 dwelling units per acre of density allowed. In other words, the R-4 Zone’s
p76 density of 40 dwelling units per acre must correspond with a height limit no
p76 lower than 4 stories and 40 feet and HCD has expressed that 42 feet is
p76 preferred to avoid constraining the density. As a result, the Housing Element
p76 Update contains Program H.1.F that requires the City to submit Measure D
p76 to the voters.
p76 If approved, Measure D would revise the height limits in both the CMC
p76 86.56.048 and the OACSP pertaining to multiple family developments in the
p76 R-4 Zone so that the maximum building height is four stories but no more
p76 than 42 feet. This revision will be in compliance with HCD’s requirements
p76 stated above.
p76 A “Yes” vote is a vote in favor of Measure D, which is a vote in favor of
p76 revising the height limits as stated above. “No” vote is a vote against
p76 Measure D, which is a vote against revising the height limits as stated above.
p76 Measure D will be approved if it receives a simple majority of “Yes” votes.

p76 ARGUMENT AGAINST MEASURE D
p76 For over fifty-four years, Coronado voters sought to protect the village
p76 character of our City. Measure D asks residents to give that protection away.
p76 The R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone governs the core of Orange
p76 Avenue between Second and Eighth Streets. Since 1972, Proposition R has
p76 capped affected building heights in that zone at three stories. Measure D
p76 would raise the limit to four stories and 42 feet.
p76 The mandate driving this measure rests on rigged math. Every eight years,
p76 California requires cities to adopt a Housing Element to accommodate
p76 housing allocations set by SANDAG. For the 2021–2029 cycle, Coronado
p76 was mandated to zone for 912 new units, requiring higher density rezoning.
p76 To calculate their mandate, SANDAG counted 10,000+ jobs on Coronado’s
p76 Navy bases, though a majority of service-members don’t reside here.
p76 Inexplicably, they also excluded existing Navy housing to account for
p76 service-members who do live on base.
p76 City Council fought hard against the SANDAG housing density mandate.
p76 However, in 2023 the City signed a settlement agreement under threat of
p76 $100,000 monthly fines and loss of local zoning authority. In March 2024,
p76 our City’s Planning Commission voted unanimously to reject the Housing
p76 Element. It was a symbolic, principled vote. City Council then reluctantly
p76 adopted the Housing Element to maintain some local zoning control and to
p76 ensure that Navy housing for sailors can offset density calculations in the
p76 current and future SANDAG mandates.
p76 Coronado voters on Measure D have a say in how the current SANDAG
p76 mandate will be implemented. Raising construction height limits is an
p76 unacceptable solution.
p76 Vote NO on Measure D. Hold the line that Coronado voters first drew in
p76 1972 — protecting our community from undesirable density and structures
p76 that don’t fit the unique character of Orange Avenue.

p77 Section 86.56.048 Exception – Building height limits for multiple-
p77 family and nonresidential development in the
p77 R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone.
p77 A.
p77 For multiple-family and nonresidential development in the
p77 R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone with a flat roof and parapet,
p77 mansard, or sloped roof less than 2:12, cable railing systems that
p77 are 90 percent transparent may extend two feet beyond the
p77 allowable building height.
p77 B.
p77 For multiple-family and nonresidential development in the
p77 R-4 Multiple-Family Residential Zone, a maximum of 20 percent of
p77 allowable lot coverage (building footprint) can be increased to 40
p77 feet in building height if the following criteria are met:
p77 1.
p77 The roof increased beyond 35 feet has a pitch equal
p77 to or greater than 2:12.
p77 2.
p77 The building volume of the main building adjoining
p77 or adjacent to the building bulk of the structure increased
p77 beyond 35 feet is reduced in a proportionate fashion so that
p77 the net result is that the total building volume of all structures
p77 is not increased.
p77 3.
p77 The increased building height shall be compatible
p77 with adjoining properties as determined by the Design
p77 Review Commission.
p77 C.
p77 For multiple-family development in the R-4 Multiple-
p77 Family Residential Zone, a maximum building height of four
p77 stories and not more than 42 feet shall apply.
